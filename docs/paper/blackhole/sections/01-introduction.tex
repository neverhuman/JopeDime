\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern tabular machine learning is divided between two paradigms that rarely compound each other's strengths. Gradient-boosted decision trees \citep{chen2016xgboost,ke2017lightgbm,prokhorenkova2018catboost} and automated ensembling pipelines \citep{feurer2015autosklearn,erickson2025tabarena} optimize per-dataset objectives from scratch on every table, discarding structural discoveries once a run finishes. Conversely, tabular in-context foundation models such as \code{TabPFN} \citep{hollmann2025tabpfn}, \code{TabICL} \citep{qu2025tabicl}, \code{LimiX-2} \citep{zhang2026limix2}, \code{TabFM}, and \code{Kumo-Tabular} amortize inference into a single forward pass, but rely on static pretraining distributions that do not absorb new corpora or retain verified per-domain transformations.

This paper investigates a direct question: \emph{when a learning system studies thousands of real tables under random non-official data partitions and amortizes verified transformations into neural weights, how accurately can it answer official benchmark splits while conditioning only on training-side rows at evaluation time?} Answering this question honestly requires separating generalization to unseen tables from retention of studied tables. Merging those regimes would conflate out-of-distribution zero-shot transfer with in-distribution consolidation; ignoring consolidation altogether leaves behind hundreds of Elo points of practical tabular accuracy on known operational schemas.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We make four concrete contributions, each bound to immutable cryptographic receipts:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Problem and kernel solution encodings (\cref{sec:encodings}).} We formulate tabular discovery around a problem encoder $\PhiP$ mapping variable-schema tables into $\zp \in \mathbb{R}^{d_p}$ and a kernel solution embedding $\PsiK$ mapping candidate transformations into $\zs \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ under a positive-definite kernel $\Kk(\zs,\zs')$, paired with a strict every-fold inner cross-validation referee achieving \BHKernelGatePrec{} held-out win precision across $3{,}639$ tables \est{}.
  \item \textbf{All-data random-split training and split-disjointness audit (\cref{sec:blackhole}).} We scale continuous multi-host neural training across \BHRFiveTables{} real tables (\BHRFiveFamilies{} families, \BHTotalDatasetsFed{} cumulative dataset episodes fed, \BHAllDataDatasetsFed{} under random-split all-data sampling) and per-table neural dossiers, backed by an exact replay audit proving zero official-split partition hits across $61{,}814$ family episodes and $155{,}937$ per-table episodes on all $91$ \code{Dev91} contexts \est{}.
  \item \textbf{Three-regime \code{Dev91} benchmark characterization (\cref{sec:results-dev91}).} Alongside \code{Kumo-Tabular-Large}, \code{LimiX-2} \citep{zhang2026limix2} (\BHEloLimiXBoard{} Elo, rank $\#2/34$ on \code{board\_34}; release checkpoint \code{ed01f7a0}, HF \code{de07b679}, which upstream found contaminated with benchmark data, \code{autogluon/tabarena\#648}, makers' audit 2026-11-11, and \code{TabArena} dropped 2026-11-16 for \code{LimiX-2-1016}), \code{Kumo-Tabular-Small}, \code{TabFM-1.0}, and \code{TabPFN-3.5}, we report separated \regfair{} (\BHEloFairReg{} pre-registered; \BHEloFairAdaptive{} adaptive), \regincorpus{} (\BHEloInCorpus{}), and \regrecall{} (\emph{absorbed: not a generalization comparison}: \BHEloRecall{} family-level; \BHEloGenTwoA{} per-table in Fit~B and \BHEloGenTwoAJoint{} in the $35$-entrant fit) Elo ladders, with $19/19$ family gains ($p = 1.9\times 10^{-6}$) from per-table consolidation \est{}.
  \item \textbf{Pre-registered public-field protocol on \code{TabArena} and \code{BeyondArena} (\cref{sec:results-public}) plus bank-disjoint validation dynamics (\cref{sec:dynamics}).} We establish non-overlapping per-regime evaluation across $44$ public regression tasks and $129$ public classification tasks, alongside training/validation loss trajectories and GPU cell-token throughput (\BHInferGpuCellsPerSec{} cells/GPU-s) \pre{}.
\end{enumerate}
