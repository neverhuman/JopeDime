\section{Cell-Token Throughput, Fit Cost, and Token Yield}
\label{sec:throughput}

A foundation or absorption architecture is practical only if its training and inference costs scale predictably with table size. We define one \emph{cell token} as a single scalar table entry $(i, j)$ processed by the neural reader's input projection across context and query rows, and report directly measured hardware rates from committed logs and timing receipts.

\paragraph{Token yield.}
We measure \emph{token yield} as verified downstream utility or Elo gained per unit of random-split compute (\cref{tab:throughput}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Family dossier yield (Look 2):} Training $19$ family dossiers to $2{,}000$ exposures/row ($61{,}814$ episodes total) lifts \code{Dev91} board mean utility by $+1.035\,u$ over the \regincorpus{} generalist stack ($1.681 \to 2.716\,u$, or \BHEloInCorpus{} $\to$ \BHEloRecall{} Elo, $+484.8$ Elo).
  \item \textbf{Per-table dossier yield (\code{GEN2A}):} Specializing one $23.7\mathrm{M}$ dossier per table ($2.1$ to $15.8$ minutes on one $24\,\mathrm{GB}$ GPU; $3.22$ hours across four GPU slots for all $81$ new tables, median $7{,}366.6$ query rows/s) lifts board mean utility by a further $+0.382\,u$ ($2.716 \to 3.098\,u$, $32\%$ lower RMSE, \BHEloGenTwoA{} Elo in Fit~B). Across all $91$ runs, \code{dup\_splits = 0} in every final record and zero runs failed or required retry \est{}.
\end{itemize}
