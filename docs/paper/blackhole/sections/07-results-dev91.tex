\section{Results on the \code{Dev91} Reference Board}
\label{sec:results-dev91}

\input{figures/fig-dev91-ladder}

The \code{Dev91} reference board comprises $91$ regression contexts grouped into $19$ domain families, scored against external entrants including \code{Kumo-Tabular-Large}, \code{LimiX-2} \citep{zhang2026limix2}, \code{Kumo-Tabular-Small}, \code{TabFM-1.0} (single and ensemble), \code{TabPFN-3.5}, \code{TabPFN-3.0}, \code{Mitra-v2}, \code{TabICL-v2}, \code{TabDPT-1.3}, \code{AutoGluon} ($300\,\mathrm{s}$ BestQuality), \code{CatBoost}, \code{XGBoost}, and \code{LightGBM}. Following \cref{sec:protocol}, \cref{tab:dev91-fits} reports separate Bradley--Terry fit blocks so Elo ratings from distinct fits and exposure regimes are never conflated: Fit~C (\code{board\_34}, the unabsorbed $34$-entrant generalization ladder including \code{LimiX-2}), Fit~D (\code{paper\_field\_35}, the $35$-entrant ladder adding absorbed \code{GEN2A}), Fit~B (the original $34$-entrant \code{GEN2A} per-table look), and Fit~A (the Look-2 \code{rev20} ladder).

\paragraph{Key findings across regimes.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{External baseline (\code{board\_34}, Fit~C).} Among entrants that saw no \code{Dev91} test row in our runs, \code{Kumo-Tabular-Large} ($2057.6$ Elo) and \code{LimiX-2}$^{\dagger}$ (\BHEloLimiXBoard{} Elo, rank $\#2/34$, family-mean $u = 1.9268$; release checkpoint \code{ed01f7a0}, HF \code{de07b679}, which upstream found contaminated with benchmark data, \code{autogluon/tabarena\#648}, makers' audit 2026-11-11, and \code{TabArena} dropped 2026-11-16 for \code{LimiX-2-1016}) are statistically indistinguishable ($12$--$0$--$7$ families and $52$--$1$--$38$ tables in favor of \code{LimiX-2}$^{\dagger}$, family-mean $\Delta u = -0.0087$, one-sided sign-flip $p = 0.574$), while \code{LimiX-2}$^{\dagger}$ outperforms \code{TabFM-1.0 Ensemble} ($17$--$0$--$2$ families, $14.7\%$ geometric-mean RMSE ratio reduction, $p = 0.0062$), \code{Kumo-Tabular-Small} ($16$--$0$--$3$, $p = 0.019$), \code{TabFM-1.0 Single} ($16$--$0$--$3$, $p = 5.8\times 10^{-4}$), \code{TabPFN-3.5} ($15$--$0$--$4$, $p = 0.012$), and our unabsorbed \regfair{} champion \code{h448} ($1813.6$ Elo; $18$--$0$--$1$ families, $77$--$0$--$14$ tables, $p = 0.0025$) \est{}.
  \item \textbf{Per-table random-split absorption (\emph{absorbed: not a generalization comparison}).} Registered after a $10$-table pilot (\code{WP1a}, ledger line 23) had been scored once and before the remaining $81$ per-table models were trained (ledger line 26), \code{GEN2A} reaches \BHEloGenTwoA{} Elo in Fit~B (\BHEloGenTwoAJoint{} in the $35$-entrant Fit~D alongside \code{LimiX-2} at \BHEloLimiXJoint{}) and improves over the $19$-family system on \textbf{all $19$ of $19$ families} ($+0.382$ mean utility, $+0.271$ clipped mean, $+0.305$ family median, $+0.258$ leave-one-family-out minimum, $32\%$ geometric-mean RMSE reduction, exact one-sided sign-flip $p = 2^{-19} = 1.9\times 10^{-6}$). Under this absorbed regime (\emph{absorbed: not a generalization comparison}), \code{GEN2A} records $88$--$0$--$3$ tables against \code{TabPFN-3.5}, $86$--$0$--$5$ against \code{Kumo-Tabular-Small}, $83$--$0$--$8$ against \code{TabFM-1.0 Single}, $79$--$0$--$12$ ($16$--$0$--$3$ families, $+1.171$ mean $u$, $69.0\%$ geometric-mean RMSE reduction) against \code{LimiX-2}, and $77$--$1$--$13$ ($16$--$0$--$3$ families) against \code{Kumo-Tabular-Large} (\cref{tab:dev91-wtl}) \est{}.
  \item \textbf{Separating absorption from unseen transfer.} Without per-family or per-table dossiers, the generalist stack scores \BHEloInCorpus{} Elo (\regincorpus{} in Fit~A), sitting $7.4$ Elo above \code{TabPFN-3.5} on the Look-2 ladder while trailing \code{TabPFN-3.5} $4$--$15$ head-to-head on families and sitting below \code{TabFM-1.0} and both \code{Kumo-Tabular} models in that same Fit~A ladder. The single pre-registered system that never saw \code{Dev91} scores \BHEloFairReg{} Elo in Fit~A ($1813.6$ in Fit~C). Thus, the $+484.8$ Elo separation within Fit~A (\code{ea4a1470}) between the unabsorbed stack (\BHEloInCorpus{}) and family dossier consolidation (\BHEloRecall{}, \emph{absorbed: not a generalization comparison}), followed by the paired $19/19$-family gain ($+0.382\,u$, $p = 1.9\times 10^{-6}$) of per-table dossiers over family dossiers, isolates what random-split weight consolidation captures \est{}.
\end{enumerate}
