\section{What the program has measured so far}
\label{app:history}

Everything in this appendix is real and comes from the JopeDime repository at the commit this draft
was written against \citep{jopedime2026repo,jopedime2026innovationzero,jopedime2026experiments}.
\est{} marks an established measurement with receipts; \pre{} marks a preliminary or non-qualifying
one. Several of these results are unfavourable to the hypothesis of this paper, and we report them
for that reason.

\subsection{Corpus and engine}

The learning-procedure corpus holds 14,987 verified runs over 3,003 dataset versions, 7,120
contexts and 1,068 parent lineages, from a real tier of 3,859 datasets \est. On the public Golden45
regression ladder, a single fixed-seed run of the program's search engine, which never sees test
labels, ranks 31st of 304 methods at Elo 1687.0 with 42 of 45 datasets covered, below tabular
foundation models such as TabPFN v3 at 1909.5 \est. Choosing the engine's best program per dataset by
its test score, the minimum over 63,108 test-scored evaluations on 42 datasets, would rank first at
2259.8; that number is an oracle envelope and must never be read as a result. The gap between the
two is the problem EurekaZero addresses: strong procedures exist in the language, and finding one
without looking at the answer is the whole difficulty.

\subsection{Frontier models at one attempt}

In the program's General Regressor Challenge, frontier models wrote one general regressor each
without seeing any data. On 64 dataset families, the four entrants rated from 1641 to 1342 on a
shared Bradley--Terry panel, and every entrant was the best of the four on some families
\pre{} \citep{jopedime2026challenge,jopedime2026writeup}. In a same-cut pilot of eighteen methods on
ten datasets that had been selected because the engine ranked first on them, the best frontier
regressor ranked first at Elo 1761.4 and the engine's searched programs fourth \pre. These results
are the reason a frontier-model control (A8) is a primary comparator in every game.

\subsection{The learned policy}

The program's first learned policy, CLOVER, has 893,957 parameters and selects among a frozen catalog
of 6,640 complete programs \est{}; it is the catalog-bound control of \cref{prop:closed}. Its
outcome head ranked freshly executed programs at chance, pairwise accuracy 0.506 [0.441, 0.568] on 32
development contexts \pre. Search seeded by the frozen policy was worse than retrieval-seeded search
on 15 of 17 complete-case training contexts, with unequal attempt budgets (1,714 against 2,082)
\pre; in the same readout, 40 of the 51 selected programs were syntactically outside the frozen
catalog, with behavioural novelty unmeasured \pre. Three continued fine-tunes of the policy, judged on 1,148
contexts, each raised a regression alert against the frozen first generation \pre. The one
favourable result is an offline shadow study in which a debiased surrogate distilled from scored runs
improved top-1 selection over two baselines; it is established only as a shadow result and does not
establish a self-improvement loop \pre.

\subsection{Training on more of the same experience}

In a matched study of 36 neural ranking models trained for a fixed 400 updates each, all training
and internal-monitor objectives ended below their initial values, but 31 of 36 internal-monitor
objectives were worse at update 400 than at update 100, against none of the training objectives.
The two predeclared ranking contrasts were $-0.1056$ $[-0.7890, +0.2870]$ and
$+0.1482$ $[-0.0231, +0.4013]$, central 98.75\% intervals with negative values favouring treatment;
both include harm, and ranking lift was not established \pre{} \citep{jopedime2026h27}.

\subsection{The state encoding}

The current policy context has 2,048 values: a 512-value native block, a 1,024-value foundation
encoding and a 512-value target encoding \est. The native block is produced by a dimension-128
checkpoint whose four pooled statistics give 512 values; it is labelled with a later version string
only to satisfy the loader, and the earlier paper's description of it as the later model is
corrected here \est. The foundation encoding is derived from a third-party pretrained tabular
regressor; its weights are provenance-pending and their redistribution is prohibited under current
terms \est. A second tabular foundation model used elsewhere in the program is not part of the state
encoding. The program's current release policy has width 512, 6 layers, 4 heads and 27,796,494
parameters \est.

\subsection{What this means for the draft}

The catalog-bound policy, its chance-level outcome head and its losses to retrieval are the reasons
the proposer of this paper has an open-support decoder, is trained on the utility of its first
proposal rather than on a ranking loss, and is compared first against retrieval and catalog
selection. The monitor-objective result is the reason the improvement rounds of \cref{sec:mechanisms}
use fresh outcomes rather than more updates on the same experience. None of these results is evidence
for EurekaZero; all of them constrain what it must show.
