\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Which innovations are games} The innovation-game hypothesis applies where four conditions
hold: the problem can be encoded as a state, candidate solutions can be written in a typed language,
an independent verifier can score them, and a budget can be declared (\cref{sec:formulation}).
Many valuable problems meet them (\cref{tab:othergames}); many do not. Problems whose quality is a
matter of taste or negotiation have no verifier. Problems whose verifier takes years, such as a
clinical trial, cannot supply the experience the method learns from. And the hardest part of much
invention is finding the problem and writing its specification, which this formulation takes as
given. We test three games and claim nothing about the rest (\cref{app:claims}, EZ-C12).

\paragraph{Verifiers can be gamed} A proposer trained on a verifier's score will exploit any gap
between the score and the intent. Typed decoders remove some exploits by construction; the verifier
runs outside the proposer's process; confirmation problems are sealed and their verifiers are not
used in training; and the CAD results are checked by a second analysis and, for a sample, by
physical test. None of this proves the absence of exploits, and each new game needs its own audit.

\paragraph{Archive novelty is not novelty} Rungs N1 to N4 of the novelty ladder are statements about
a frozen archive, which is large but not the literature. Rungs N5 and N6 add interventions, prior-work
review and replication, and still cannot prove that no one has had the idea before.

\paragraph{Sealed is not unbounded} Confirmation families are unseen, but they come from the same
generators and sources as training families. Problems far from that distribution may behave like the
regimes in \cref{sec:heterogeneity}, where the first proposal's advantage disappears.

\paragraph{Cost and reproducibility} The fixed costs in \cref{sec:compute} are within reach of an
academic laboratory but not of every one, and the learning-procedure adapter depends on third-party
pretrained encoders whose redistribution terms we do not control (\cref{app:model}). The
EurekaZero-Native arm exists so that the central claims do not depend on them.

\paragraph{Dual use and inventorship} A system that proposes working molecules, controllers or parts
in one step lowers the cost of harmful designs as well as useful ones \citep{urbina2022dualuse}. We
restrict the released games to the three studied here and gate verifier access for any future game
with a plausible misuse route. Under the USPTO's revised inventorship guidance, only natural persons
can be named as inventors and AI systems are treated as tools \citep{uspto2025inventorship}; who
conceives an invention first proposed by EurekaZero is a legal question we do not answer.

\paragraph{This draft} Every EurekaZero result above is synthetic. The draft fixes the claims, the
protocol and the analyses in advance so that the experiments can confirm or refute them; the
experiments may do either.
