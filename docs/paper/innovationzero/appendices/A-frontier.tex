\section{The frontier comparison in full: where each entrant wins}
\label{app:frontier}

This appendix unpacks \cref{fig:frontier}a into the records it summarizes. Every number is derived
from two archived files, \code{artifacts/benchmark/match-matrix.txt} (one row per family, one column
per pair of entrants, holding the first entrant's seed-averaged match score) and
\code{artifacts/benchmark/RESULTS.json}, by \code{scripts/derive-frontier.sh}; the build regenerates
the plotted tables from those files and fails if they differ from the committed copies in
\code{data/}. The panel, protocol and limits are those of \cref{sec:u64} \pre.

\paragraph{Head-to-head} The six pairings are decisive in direction and never unanimous
(\cref{fig:frontier-wins}a). Opus5p5\_Extra wins the majority of seeds against Gemini4\_argon\_high
in 39 of 64 families, against GPT6p1\_Sol\_Max in 51 and against GPT6\_Astra\_Max in 56;
Gemini beats Sol in 45 and Astra in 49; Sol beats Astra in 47. No family produced a tie on a
majority of seeds. The weakest entrant still wins at least eight families against the strongest.

\paragraph{Every family} Panel (c) shows the same records family by family. Opus is the best of the
four in 30 families, Gemini in 20, Sol in 7 and Astra in 5; one family is shared by Opus and Sol and
one by Opus, Gemini and Sol. Opus wins all three of its matches on every seed in 24 families,
Gemini in 16, Sol in 6 and Astra in 1. At the other end Astra loses every match on every seed in 30
families, Sol in 8, Opus in 3 and Gemini in 2. A portfolio that could choose the right one of these
four general algorithms for each family would beat each of them; choosing it requires reading the
data, which is the step InnovationZero proposes to learn.

\paragraph{Magnitude} Panel (b) repeats \cref{tab:magnitude}. The ranking by wins and the ranking by
error magnitude agree only at the bottom: Astra's errors are 30--35\% larger than Opus's and Sol's,
with intervals that exclude parity, while the top three cannot be separated on magnitude. Sol's
geometric-mean error is the lowest of the four at the point estimate.

\paragraph{Cost} \Cref{tab:frontier-cost} gives the resource receipts behind \cref{fig:frontier}d.
Fit and prediction times are totals over 576 cells, each isolated to one CPU; the first three
entrants ran on one host and Gemini on another, so its times are not a controlled comparison.
