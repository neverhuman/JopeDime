## 1. Executive summary

### 1.1 The diagnosis in four sentences

1. **The shipped search is too small and too blind to show invention.** `jope pipeline run` runs a population of 12 with 4 elites for a default of 64 evaluations on one cut (`crates/jope-pipeline/src/search.rs:92-99`, `:241-248`; `cli.rs:65`), choosing parents uniformly from the survivors on a single island by default (`cli.rs:37`) — about seven to eight generations of mostly local mutation. With a budget that small, *where you start* dominates, which is exactly what EXP-008 measured: retrieval-seeded GP beat ordinary GP on 16 of 17 contexts, and policy-seeded GP lost to retrieval-seeded on 15 of 17 (non-qualifying, unequal attempts).
2. **The neural policy is a cataloguer, not an inventor, and it has not learned from GP.** The CLOVER PolicyLM decodes through a fixed trie of catalogued programs (6,640 in the v1 catalogue that `jope-learning/src/support/version.rs:93-94` pins when `require_v1_catalog` is set; 6,692 in the extended support EI-1 used, C186) while 40 of 51 GP champions in EXP-008 lie outside that catalogue; the accepted step-60000 policy sees the whole dataset as one prefix token and scores programs with an order-blind mean of token embeddings (`jope-policy/src/model.rs:556-568`); its q-head ranks fresh programs at chance (0.506, C76); and every KL-anchored continuation fine-tune so far regressed or was inconclusive (EXP-006/007, EXP-013–017).
3. **Labels are scarce because every label costs a real search on a real dataset.** The best measured seeded-search comparison (EXP-008) covers 17 complete contexts; the CLOVER ratchet hard-codes `policy_optimizer_updates: 0` (`clover/expansion.rs:572`, `:1461`); no live S3 training run has been made (README).
4. **The evidence system is excellent and should be the asset, not the obstacle.** Receipts, hash-chained ledgers, conditional S3 writes, the F10 promotion rule, the Gate F arena, SPQ-1 round qualification and the claim ledger are stronger than most research codebases. What is missing is a loop that produces *many cheap exact labels*, a policy that can *use* them, and a search strong enough to be worth distilling.

### 1.2 The thesis

> **Make labels abundant and exact, make the policy an editor that sees columns, residuals and memory, make the expert search strong and cheap, reward the agent for beating its own history, and spend real held-out data only on qualification.**

Concretely, JopeDime becomes a *Kernel Game* (§6) with five games: a Build game (edit a typed program; the evaluator is a chance node), Ranked self-play (beat your own α-percentile on similar problems), a Dream game (a generator invents regression problems from hidden programs, so the answer is known), a Library game (compress winners into new primitives with `jope-motifs`), and — later — a League.

### 1.5 What this spec does *not* propose

- No MuZero-style learned dynamics: edits have exact transitions.
- No deep MCTS before a Gumbel root search has beaten `modern_gp` at equal measured fits.
- No new Python; no second production trainer; no new top-level crate until a stable shared abstraction exists.
- No claim of a Golden45 record from this work; that is a separate preregistered campaign (§17, wave EXP-030).

### 3.3 Blind spots shared by all five

1. **No reading of the code at HEAD.** All five disclaim this; it still costs them: K1 (credentials already fixed), K4 (the KL guard cannot trip), K5 (prereg `B = 8` vs `ELITES = 4`), K8 (stage cache unused), K9 (decay on LayerNorm, constant LR), K10–K11 (head-list drift, C++/JS head coverage).
2. **No source of abundant labels.** Each plan's learning data comes from real searches on real datasets, so each plan is bottlenecked on the expensive, selection-biased evidence that has not moved the policy so far. Synthetic hidden-program tasks appear only as a late P2 "curriculum" item in four of them.
3. **No curriculum mechanism for a one-player game.** None uses Ranked Reward or any other way to get AlphaZero's "the opponent improves as you do" property.
4. **The policy's information diet is never questioned.** None notices that the accepted policy sees the dataset as one prefix token or that the CLOVER policy is bounded by a 6,640-program trie; none proposes giving the policy the residual of the current program.
5. **The EI-0..EI-4 and EXP-001 evidence is not used to design the optimizer.** One (rank 1) mentions auditing "constant learning-rate/plateau-to-floor behavior"; none proposes plasticity countermeasures.
6. **"Clear out low-value history" is not designed.** All five protect evidence; none says *how to decide* what to delete, none notices that the veox buckets are versioned with object lock (so deletes do not free storage without non-current-version rules), and none builds on the existing `jope_retention` classifier.
7. **Repository debt is mentioned, not reduced.** None addresses the 229 K-line campaigns crate, its uncompiled `campaign.rs`, the benchmark-invalid `test_adaptive` tree, or the 257 K lines of research Python under `deploy/` against ADR-0006.
8. **Self-scores are not evidence.** The five self-scores (96–98) exceed every source they rank. This document does not score itself; judge it with the same rubric.

## 4. Invariants

These hold for every component in this spec. Most already hold in the repository; the point is to keep them true as the loop closes.

1. **Measured and predicted are different types.** `Measured<T>` comes only from an evaluator receipt; `Predicted<T>` comes from any model. No function that admits evidence, trains a value target, moves a pointer or writes a claim accepts `Predicted<T>` (§12.2).
2. **Learning never reads held-out evidence.** Outer test folds, `meta_test` families, `sealed` buckets and `jope.pipeline-test-score/v1` records are outside every training and search input closure. The existing `feeds_selection: false` field becomes a type-level guarantee.
3. **Every adaptive decision is logged with its alternatives.** Parent choice, operator, fidelity, seed source, stop: the chosen action, the legal-set digest, and the behaviour probability (or the deterministic rule's version).
4. **Raw outcome vectors are immutable; scalar rewards are views.** RMSE per cut, R², failure class, CPU-seconds and wall-seconds are stored; `g`, ranked reward and any utility are recomputable views keyed by their definition's SHA-256.
5. **Every claim of improvement is paired, clustered and at equal resources.** Parent-cluster bootstrap (`pools::bootstrap::cluster_bootstrap_ci`), the F10 tie rule (`|ln| ≤ 1e-12`), equal fits *and* reported CPU-seconds.
6. **Exact transitions are exploited, not learned.** Edits are pure functions; only measurement is uncertain.
7. **Throughput is a first-class metric.** Fits per CPU-hour and fits per qualified improvement are reported for every wave.
8. **Retention never breaks evidence closure.** Anything reachable from an accepted pointer, an established claim, a preregistration or a promotion decision is kept; everything else is kept *in proportion to its value*, with its inclusion probability recorded.
9. **Summaries are compiled, never authored.** Cards, beliefs and `STATE.md` are deterministic functions of evidence; prose explanations are labelled as such.
10. **One implementation per concern.** One production trainer (`jope-engine`), one search kernel with profiles, one operator registry, one evaluator authority, one S3 store abstraction (`jope-datasets::store`).

## 5. Self-play and program-search: what actually works, and what transfers to the Kernel Game

This section is the research base the design stands on. Each row ends with the one lesson JopeDime should take, because most of these systems differ from JopeDime in a way that changes the right design.

### 5.1 The AlphaZero family, read as engineering

| System | Core loop | What made it work | Lesson for the Kernel Game |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AlphaGo Zero** (Silver et al., *Nature* 2017) | Self-play with MCTS → train policy toward visit counts and value toward game outcome → new network plays itself | MCTS is a **policy-improvement operator**: the search distribution is strictly better than the raw network, so distilling it improves the network. Two-player zero-sum gives an automatic curriculum (your opponent improves exactly as fast as you do). | The improvement operator must be *provably better than the raw policy at equal cost* before you distil it. JopeDime has no opponent, so the curriculum has to be manufactured (§6.3, §6.4). |
| **AlphaZero** (Silver et al., arXiv:1712.01815) | Same, one algorithm for chess/shogi/Go, continuous training (no gating) | Simulator is **exact and free**; a game ends in ≤ a few hundred moves with a scalar outcome. | JopeDime's "simulator" (the evaluator) is exact but **expensive** (C85: ≤ ~1,024 evals/h preliminary) and **noisy across cuts**. Every design choice must count measured leaves, not tree nodes. |
| **Expert Iteration** (Anthony et al., arXiv:1705.08439) | Apprentice (network) guides expert (tree search); expert's choices train apprentice | Named the pattern: *fast* policy + *slow* search, alternating. | This is exactly "neural seeds generation 0, GP improves, GP results train the network". Our GP is the expert. The loop is only closed when the apprentice is retrained from expert output and then shown to make the expert better. |
| **MuZero** (Schrittwieser et al., arXiv:1911.08265) | Learns a latent dynamics model and plans in it | Needed because Atari/Go rules were not given to the planner. | **Do not build a MuZero.** Program edits have *exact, known* transitions (apply an edit to an S-expression). Only the *evaluation* is unknown. Learn a value/outcome model, not a dynamics model. |
| **Sampled MuZero** (Hubert et al., arXiv:2104.06303) | Plans over a *sampled* subset of a huge action space with importance correction | Makes tree search work when actions cannot be enumerated. | The edit action space (site × operator × parameters) is combinatorial. Sample K legal edits from the policy at each node and correct the improved-policy target for the sampling (§6.6). |
| **Gumbel AlphaZero/MuZero** (Danihelka et al., ICLR 2022) | Gumbel-top-k sampling without replacement at the root + sequential halving; "completed Q" improved policy | **Guaranteed policy improvement even with very few simulations** (as low as 2). | The right root algorithm when each simulation that reaches a leaf costs a real fit. Use it before any deeper tree. |
| **Stochastic MuZero** (Antonoglou et al., ICLR 2022) | Afterstates + chance nodes | Separates the agent's choice from environment randomness. | Model "evaluate this program at fidelity f on cut c" as a **chance node** over noisy outcomes. Choosing *whether and how expensively to measure* becomes an agent action; the result is chance (§6.2). |
| **AlphaTensor** (Fawzi et al., *Nature* 610, 2022) | Single-player "TensorGame"; reward −1 per step; **synthetic demonstrations** (random decompositions sampled backwards) mixed into training | The game was *invented* so that an algorithm is a sequence of moves; training data was generated by sampling solutions first and building the problem from them. | **The single most transferable idea in this document.** Sample a hidden program, build a dataset from it, and train the policy to recover it. Unlimited exact supervision, no sealed data touched (§6.4). |
| **AlphaDev** (Mankowitz et al., *Nature* 618, 2023) | Single-player assembly game; MCTS; correctness + latency reward | Two-part reward: hard correctness gate, then measured latency. | Constraint-first reward: legality/parity/leakage are gates, RMSE and cost are utility. |

### 5.2 Evolutionary and LLM-driven program search

| System | Mechanism | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **AutoML-Zero** (Real et al., arXiv:2003.03384) | Evolves whole ML algorithms (setup/predict/learn) from basic math ops; regularized (aging) evolution; **functional-equivalence checking** (hash outputs on a fixed probe to skip re-evaluating behaviourally identical programs) | Closest precedent to "invent models from scratch to code". Aging evolution beats truncation for robustness. FEC is a cheap throughput win whose size here must be measured (§8.8, EXP-020). |
| **Lion / Symbolic Discovery of Optimization Algorithms** (Chen et al., arXiv:2302.06675) | Program search over optimizers; abstract execution to prune invalid programs; **funnel selection** (promote only programs that survive progressively larger proxy tasks); simplification of winners | Funnel selection is multi-fidelity *for generalisation*, not just cost. Apply to promotion: inner fold → more folds → new datasets of the same family. |
| **FunSearch** (Romera-Paredes et al., *Nature* 625, 2024) | LLM proposes code; evaluator scores; islands database; prompt with best k programs | Island model with periodic reset of weakest islands keeps diversity. Evaluator is the trust anchor. |
| **AlphaEvolve** (Novikov et al., arXiv:2506.13131; DeepMind impact update May 2026) | LLM ensemble edits whole files as diffs; program database (MAP-Elites + islands); evaluation cascade | Diff-based edits on a parent beat whole-program regeneration; cascade of cheap→expensive evaluators. |
| **ShinkaEvolve** (Lange et al., arXiv:2509.19349, ICLR 2026) | Sample-efficient: novelty rejection sampling (embedding similarity) before evaluation, bandit over mutation LLMs, adaptive parent sampling | **Reject near-duplicates before paying for a fit**; a bandit over operator sources. Both are cheap and directly applicable. |
| **Darwin Gödel Machine** (Zhang et al., arXiv:2505.22954) | Self-modifying agent archive; open-ended branching from any archived ancestor | Keep an archive and allow branching from non-elite ancestors; stepping stones matter. Keep the evaluator outside the modifiable region. |
| **MAP-Elites** (Mouret & Clune, arXiv:1504.04909), **Go-Explore** (Ecoffet et al., arXiv:1901.10995), **Novelty search** (Lehman & Stanley 2011) | Archive of best-per-behaviour-cell; return-then-explore | Quality-diversity archives retain stepping stones that a top-k population discards. |
| **Lexicase / ε-lexicase** (Spector 2012; La Cava et al., GECCO 2016), **down-sampled lexicase** (Hernandez et al., GECCO 2019) | Select parents by filtering on random orderings of individual cases (folds/row-groups) | Preserves specialists that win on subsets; down-sampling cuts its cost and improves generalisation. |
| **Linear scaling** (Keijzer, EuroGP 2003); **nonlinear least-squares constant fitting** (Kommenda et al., GPEM 2020; Operon; PySR arXiv:2305.01582) | Fit the affine output scale and the numeric constants analytically/by Levenberg–Marquardt instead of by mutation | The largest known single win in symbolic regression GP. Structure from evolution, constants from optimization. |
| **Semantic backpropagation** (Pawlak, Wieloch & Krawiec, IEEE TEVC 2015) | Compute the *desired* output of a subtree from the target and the rest of the program; pick a library subtree that matches | A principled "edit the part that is wrong" operator; pairs naturally with residual-conditioned policies (§9.4). |
| **ALPS / age-fitness Pareto** (Hornby 2006; Schmidt & Lipson 2010), **regularized evolution** (Real et al., arXiv:1802.01548) | Protect young lineages from being out-competed by mature ones | Fixes premature convergence; JopeDime's migration, when islands are enabled, sends top-1 everywhere (§8.1, §8.7). |
| **DreamCoder** (Ellis et al., arXiv:2006.08381), **Stitch** (Bowers et al., arXiv:2211.16605), **babble** (Cao et al., arXiv:2212.04596) | Wake (solve), sleep-abstraction (compress solutions into a library), sleep-dreaming (train the recogniser on fantasies sampled from the library) | Three things at once: library learning grows the language; dreaming generates synthetic training tasks; the recognition model is trained on both. `jope-motifs` is the start of this (§7.7). |

### 5.3 Training methods (what to use when)

| Method | When it is the right tool | JopeDime verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Supervised imitation / **Expert Iteration distillation** (cross-entropy to search output) | You have a search that is verifiably better than the policy | **Primary.** Safest, already implemented as `ce`/`L_PI` terms. |
| **AWR / AWAC** (Peng et al., arXiv:1910.00177; Nair et al., arXiv:2006.09359) | Off-policy data with measured advantages | Use for the archive (past measured programs) with exp(A/τ) weights, clipped. |
| **ReST-EM / STaR** (Singh et al., arXiv:2312.06585; Zelikman et al., arXiv:2203.14465) | Generate → filter by verifier → fine-tune; iterate | This *is* the flywheel. Its failure mode is mode collapse onto easy wins — exactly what the EI-0..EI-4 KL alarms show (§2.3). |
| **PPO** (arXiv:1707.06347) | Dense, cheap rewards | Not here: rewards are sparse and expensive; value function would be undertrained. |
| **RLOO** (Ahmadian et al., arXiv:2402.14740), **GRPO** (DeepSeekMath, arXiv:2402.03300), **POMO** shared baseline (Kwon et al., arXiv:2010.16011) | Several samples per context; baseline = mean of the others | Good fit for K seeds per context **if** behaviour log-probs are exact. Already implemented offline (#287). |
| **PKPO — pass@k policy optimisation** (Walder & Karkhanis, arXiv:2505.15201) | You care about the **best of k** samples, not the average | **Exactly the generation-0 objective**: seeds are judged by the best program GP finds from them. Already implemented offline (#287). Promote it to the primary seed objective (§10.2). |
| **DPO / IPO** (arXiv:2305.18290) | Pairwise preferences without a reward model | Useful as a pairwise ranking regulariser on measured pairs; offline C11 exists (#286). Secondary. |
| **Distributional value** (QR-DQN, Dabney et al., arXiv:1710.10044) | Heavy-tailed, multimodal returns | Program improvements are heavy-tailed; predict quantiles, act on upper quantiles for exploration and lower quantiles for promotion. |
| **Prioritized replay** (Schaul et al., arXiv:1511.05952); **PLR** (Jiang et al., arXiv:2010.03934); **PAIRED** (Dennis et al., arXiv:2012.02096); **POET** (Wang et al., arXiv:1901.01753) | Curricula and replay that focus on learnable, high-regret tasks | Drive task sampling and hidden-program difficulty by measured regret (§6.4). |
| **Ranked Reward** (Laterre et al., arXiv:1807.01672) | Single-player optimisation where you want AlphaZero-style self-play | Reward = +1 if this episode beats the α-percentile of the agent's own recent results on comparable instances, −1 otherwise. **This is how JopeDime gets real "self-play" without an opponent** (§6.3). |
| **PSRO / league** (Lanctot et al., arXiv:1711.00832; AlphaStar, *Nature* 575, 2019) | Non-transitive multi-agent games | Only after ≥ 3 promoted policies exist and the payoff matrix shows non-transitivity. Until then it is ceremony. |

### 5.4 Optimizers and continual training (the flywheel is continual learning)

| Topic | Evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| **AdamW** (Loshchilov & Hutter, arXiv:1711.05101) | Default for transformers | Keep as baseline. |
| **Plateau LR to a floor** | JopeDime EXP-001: LR halved 9 times to the `1e-6` floor at step 5,677 and stayed there for 12,096 more updates while validation L6 sat flat (`SYNTHESIS_AND_NEXT_EXPERIMENTS.md` WH1). | **Retire.** 68% of that run's updates were spent at a learning rate that cannot move the model. |
| **Warmup–Stable–Decay (WSD)** (MiniCPM, arXiv:2404.06395) | Lets you branch a decayed checkpoint from a constant-LR trunk at any time | Ideal for a flywheel: keep a stable trunk, decay a short branch per candidate child. |
| **Schedule-Free AdamW** (Defazio et al., arXiv:2405.15682) | No schedule horizon needed | Run as an ablation arm against WSD; same motivation. |
| **Muon** (Liu et al., arXiv:2502.16982) | ~2× compute efficiency for 2-D hidden matrices in LLM pretraining | Ablation only, on hidden matrices; AdamW on embeddings/heads/norms. Not the bottleneck today. |
| **Loss of plasticity** (Dohare et al., *Nature* 632, 2024); **warm-start generalisation gap** (Ash & Adams, arXiv:1910.08475, "shrink and perturb") | Networks trained continually on shifting data lose the ability to learn; warm-started nets generalise worse than fresh ones | **Directly relevant to EI-0..EI-4.** Each flywheel child is a warm start of the last. Use shrink-and-perturb at round boundaries, periodic head re-initialisation, and a scheduled "re-distil from scratch" generation (§10.5). |
| **EMA/Polyak weights** | Smoother served policy | Serve the EMA; train the raw weights. |

### 5.5 Tabular foundation models (the competitive bar)

TabPFN (Hollmann et al., *Nature* 637, 2025) and its successors define the bar on the Golden45 ladder: TabFM Ensemble Elo 1934.9, TabPFN v3 1909.5, against Jope Dime Regressor 1687.0 (rank 31, 42/45). Prior Labs shipped TabPFN-3 in May 2026 (up to 1M rows, 200 features) and TabPFN-3.5 in September 2026 (up to 20,000 features). TabICL (Qu et al., arXiv:2502.05564) is a strong open alternative. Two consequences:

1. **Context encoding.** The accepted policy's context already contains a 1,024-value block from the in-context Starlight foundation encoder (`jope-encoders/src/foundation.rs`) next to 512 Hyperion and 512 target-encoder values. The problem is not the encoder; it is that all of it is pooled into one vector before the policy attends to it (§9.1). `deploy/dl-trainonly` quarantines a TabPFN-3.5 encoder as research-only.
2. **A foundation model is a *primitive*, not a competitor.** A program `(pipe X (fm_oof) (ridge-on-residual …))` can only tie or beat the FM on inner CV when the residual stage helps. That is the shortest path from rank 31 to the top of the ladder, and it is a program the GP can discover (§7.5).

### 5.6 The eight lessons, condensed

1. Distil a search only after proving it beats the raw policy **at equal measured cost**.
2. Exact transitions → AlphaZero-style planning with a learned *value*, not MuZero.
3. Expensive leaves → Gumbel root + sequential halving, not thousands of simulations.
4. No opponent → Ranked Reward against your own history, plus a generator that invents tasks with known answers.
5. Search quality is bounded by evaluation throughput; dedup by behaviour before fitting.
6. Constants by optimisation, structure by evolution, abstractions by compression.
7. Continual fine-tuning loses plasticity; plan for it.
8. The best tabular models are foundation models; make them building blocks of programs.

## 6. The Kernel Game — the formal design

The user's framing is the right one: *given a tabular regression problem, play the game of proposing the best algorithm kernel*. This section turns that sentence into five games with exact states, actions, rewards and data flows. Each game reuses an existing JopeDime component; none needs a new trainer.

```text
                     ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                     │ G5 League (later): historical policies       │
                     │    matched-budget payoff matrix, PSRO        │
                     └───────────────▲──────────────────────────────┘
                                     │ promoted children
┌─────────────────────┐   tasks with │ known answers   ┌──────────────────────┐
│ G3 Dream game       │──────────────┼────────────────▶│ G1 Build game        │
│ Generator invents   │◀─ regret ────┼─────────────────│ Solver edits a typed │
│ hidden programs     │              │                 │ program; evaluator   │
└─────────────────────┘              │                 │ is the chance node   │
                                     │                 └──────────▲───────────┘
┌─────────────────────┐              │                            │ reward
│ G4 Library game     │── new macros ┘   ┌────────────────────────┴───────────┐
│ compress winners    │─────────────────▶│ G2 Ranked self-play: beat your own │
│ into motifs         │                  │ α-percentile on similar contexts   │
└─────────────────────┘                  └────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 6.1 G1 — the Build game (single-player, exact transitions, stochastic measurement)

**Context `c`.** The *train-role* rows of a dataset (features `X`, target `y`), its fold plan (`prepare.rs`, 5 folds, FNV-1a assignment), its Hyperion/encoder embedding, cheap meta-features, and the identity of the evaluator. Sealed test rows and `meta_test` families are not part of `c`, ever.

**State `s = (c, p, M, b, A)`.**

| Component | Content | Source today |
|---|---|---|
| `p` | current canonical program (`GenomeIr` / S-expression), possibly with one typed hole during construction | `feat_math::genome` |
| `M` | measurement history: set of `(program_sha256, fidelity, cut, outcome)` | pipeline observations (`observations_sha256`) |
| `b` | remaining budget **vector**: fits, CPU-seconds, wall-seconds | `Budget` in `search.rs` (evaluations, seconds) |
| `A` | archive view: retrieved neighbours, QD cell occupancy, motif table version | new (§8.4), `jope-motifs` |

**Decision actions.** All are typed and legality-masked before the policy normalises:

| Action family | Parameters | Exists today as |
|---|---|---|
| `Reset(q)` | adopt a retrieved / archived / policy-generated program `q` | seeds, `--incumbent`, retrieval arm |
| `Insert(i, op, θ)` | insert a transform stage at position `i` | `mutate_canonical_pipeline` (untyped, random site) |
| `Delete(i)` / `Replace(i, op, θ)` | remove / swap a stage | same |
| `SetParam(i, k, v)` | change one parameter | same (random) |
| `SetHead(h, θ)` | swap the model head | same |
| `SetTarget(t)` | target transform | `feat_math::model::fit` target-transform step |
| `Cross(q, i, j)` | splice a compatible segment from archive program `q` | `crossover_canonical_pipelines` (untyped) |
| `Motif(m, i)` | insert macro `motif_*` at `i` | `jope-motifs` (quarantined) |
| `Refine` | fit continuous constants by optimisation, not mutation | `evolve` refinement, kernel2 `refinement` |
| `Simplify` | apply exact rewrites | e-graph code in `advanced_v3` |
| `Measure(f)` | spend budget to observe `p` at fidelity `f` | `evaluate` (`search.rs:507`) |
| `Stop` | end the episode | budget exhaustion |

**Transitions.** Every edit action is a *pure function* of `(p, action)`: the next program is known exactly. `Measure(f)` leads to a **chance node** whose outcome is the evaluator's result (Stochastic-MuZero afterstate). This is why JopeDime needs a learned *value*, not a learned *dynamics* model.

**Fidelity ladder `f`** (each is a chance node with known cost):

| Rung | What runs | Typical cost | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `F0 static` | parse, type/effect check, cost bound | ~0 | reject illegal |
| `F1 probe` | fit on ≤ 512 train rows of cut 0, score on ≤ 256 inner rows, plus a behaviour fingerprint on a fixed probe | ~1–5% of a full fit | FEC dedup, screening |
| `F2 cut0` | one cut, full train rows | 1 fit | current shipped default (`--cuts 1`) |
| `F3 cuts` | 3 cuts | 3 fits | race survivors |
| `F4 all` | all 5 cuts | 5 fits | the only rung allowed to name an episode champion |

**Terminal outcome.** The episode returns `p* = argmin` mean held-out RMSE over programs measured at `F4`. If none reached `F4`, the episode returns the incumbent (no-regression by construction, as the bootstrap genome guarantees today).

**Utility (stored as a vector, scalarised only as a view).** For context `c` and reference program `p_ref` (best of: bootstrap `(pipe X (ridge))`, retrieval top-1, the context's high-water mark):

```text
g(p*, c) = ln RMSE_F4(p_ref, c) − ln RMSE_F4(p*, c)        (positive = better; the same paired log
                                                              statistic SPQ-1 and F10 already use)
cost(e)  = (fits, cpu_seconds, wall_seconds) consumed by the episode
R_view   = g − λ_cpu · cpu_seconds / b0_cpu                 (declared per experiment; never stored as truth)
```

### 6.2 Why the evaluator is a chance node, and what that buys

Treating measurement as an action with a cost and a random outcome makes three things fall out of the same tree:

1. **Multi-fidelity is planning, not a heuristic.** Whether to promote a candidate from `F2` to `F4` is a decision with a value of information; sequential halving at the root is the closed-form special case.
2. **Noise across cuts is modelled honestly.** A single lucky cut is a single draw from a chance node, so the backed-up value of "this program" averages over cuts instead of trusting the max. This is the exact failure the pipeline docs warn about: rounds that "select on reused folds".
3. **Surrogate predictions have a type.** In planning, an unmeasured leaf is valued by the value model's predictive distribution. That number is `Predicted<g>`; only a chance-node outcome is `Measured<g>`. The two types never convert (§12.2).

### 6.3 G2 — Ranked self-play: AlphaZero-style outcomes without an opponent

A single-player optimisation game has no opponent to make the task harder as the agent improves. **Ranked Reward** (Laterre et al., 2018) fixes this:

```text
For each context family k (parent cluster from the existing parent_of_context maps, or k-means over
the context embedding when no parent map exists), keep a FIFO buffer B_k of the last W episode
utilities g produced by the current policy generation (W = 256 by default).

    r(episode) = +1  if g > Q_α(B_k)
               = −1  if g < Q_α(B_k)
               = ±1 uniformly at random if g == Q_α(B_k)       (tie rule from the R2 paper)
    α = 0.75 initially; raise to 0.9 as the win-rate on B_k exceeds 0.6 for 3 consecutive rounds.
```

- The value head predicts `P(r = +1 | s)`, a stationary-enough binary target that rises automatically as the policy improves — the property that made AlphaGo Zero's curriculum free.
- The raw `g` is still stored; `r` is a *view* keyed by `(policy_generation, α, W, B_k snapshot sha256)` so any later analysis can recompute it.
- The ranked reward is used for **training signals only**. Promotion of a child policy still uses the existing Gate F arena (or F10 for the step-60000 lineage) and SPQ-1 paired log-ratio statistics on held-out evidence (§11).

This is the precise sense in which JopeDime will be able to say "self-play": *the policy is rewarded for beating its own recent best on similar problems, and the bar moves as it improves.*

### 6.4 G3 — The Dream game: a generator invents problems whose answers are known

This is the highest-leverage idea in this specification and the one none of the five reviewed specs makes central. It is AlphaTensor's synthetic demonstrations and DreamCoder's dreaming, adapted to regression.

**The asymmetry it exploits.** Finding a good program for a dataset is hard. Making a dataset for which a given program is good is easy: sample the program first.

**Generator.** A hidden program `h = (T, f_w, ε)` is sampled from a generator `G_φ`:

| Part | Sampling | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `T` | a transform pipeline drawn from the grammar (`random_canonical_pipeline` today; later the policy itself, or a learned generator `G_φ`) | complexity `k` = number of stages, controls difficulty |
| `f_w` | a *parameterised* member of a head family with sampled parameters: sparse linear `w`, random oblique-hinge sum, random depth-`d` tree with random leaf values, random-Fourier-feature ridge with random weights, low-order polynomial interactions | parameters are sampled, not fitted |
| `X` | **real train-role feature matrices** from the S3 dataset catalog (keeps marginals, missingness, correlation and scale realistic), optionally column-subsampled; or a Gaussian copula fitted to them | never sealed families, never test rows |
| `ε` | Gaussian, Student-t(ν=3), or heteroscedastic `σ(x)`; noise scale chosen to hit a target signal-to-noise ratio `ρ ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 5, 20}` | `σ` is known exactly |

`y = f_w(T(X)) + ε`. The task is the pair `(X, y)`; its folds are built by the ordinary `prepare.rs` code path.

**What the known answer gives you** (none of which a real dataset can give):

1. **An absolute floor.** The Bayes RMSE is `σ` (or `E[σ(x)²]^{1/2}`). Normalised excess risk `ξ = (RMSE² − σ²) / (RMSE_ridge² − σ²)` is comparable across every synthetic task.
2. **An oracle.** Refit `h`'s *structure* on the train rows (`feat_math::fit` on the S-expression of `T` + head family) to get the best achievable *with this grammar*: `regret = ln RMSE(solver) − ln RMSE(oracle refit)`.
3. **Exact supervision.** The hidden program (and every program within ε of the oracle's behaviour on a probe) is a correct label for the policy. The policy can be trained by plain cross-entropy toward `h`, with no search in the loop at all.
4. **Calibration data for the value model.** Thousands of `(context, program, outcome)` triples per CPU-hour, spread over a controllable difficulty range.

**The two-player game.** The generator is rewarded by the solver's regret, but must stay solvable:

```text
Generator objective (PAIRED-style):   maximise  regret_protagonist − regret_antagonist
  protagonist = current solver policy + search at the standard budget
  antagonist  = oracle refit of h (known structure), or a 10× budget GP run when h uses
                operators outside the solver's grammar (misspecified tasks)
Task replay (PLR-style):  keep a buffer of generated tasks scored by the protagonist's
                          positive value loss; resample high-regret, still-learnable tasks
```

Practical default: start with **no learned generator at all** — sample `h` from the grammar with a complexity schedule, keep a PLR buffer, and add `G_φ` only after the replay buffer stops producing learning progress.

**Guarding against sim-to-real drift.**

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Real data is not generated by our grammar | 30% of hidden programs use operators the solver *cannot* express (smooth interactions, thresholds on unseen features, periodic terms) so the solver learns graceful approximation, not exact recall |
| The solver overfits to generator artefacts | every dream round is judged on **real train-role contexts** (inner CV) before its data is admitted to training; a dream-trained child that does not improve real inner-CV utility is rejected |
| Distribution shift of `X` | `X` is always real train-role data or a copula of it |
| Leakage | the generator reads only datasets whose S3 role is `train`; the S3 IAM policy for the dream worker denies `sealed/` and `meta_test` prefixes (§13.6) |

**Why this changes the flywheel's economics.** Today every training label costs a real search on a real dataset, and the best measured evidence (EXP-008) has 17 complete contexts. A dream worker produces labelled `(task, program)` pairs at the cost of one or two fits each, and the label is exact. The scarce real data is then spent where it belongs: **qualification**, not supervision.

### 6.5 G4 — The Library game: inventing primitives

`jope-motifs` already mines 2–6-stage fragments from independently qualified programs and expands `motif_*` references before canonicalisation, with every entry quarantined and no promotion path (`crates/jope-motifs/src/lib.rs`). Turn that into a game:

```text
State   : current grammar G_t (primitives + admitted motifs), corpus of qualified winners W
Action  : propose motif m (a fragment with typed holes for its parameters)
Reward  : Δ description length of W under G_t ∪ {m}       (Stitch/babble compression objective)
          × measured search-efficiency gain: AUC_best-so-far(G_t ∪ {m}) / AUC(G_t)
          on held-out training contexts at equal budget  (must be > 1 with CI)
Admission: quarantined → validated (expansion exactness, determinism, emitter absence — exists)
          → search-enabled in research lanes → promoted after a preregistered A/B (new)
```

Grammar growth changes the token vocabulary. Each admitted motif gets a new grammar version; policies trained before it see the motif only through its expansion. This keeps old checkpoints valid.

### 6.6 Search inside G1: the progression and its exact algorithms

The search is the *expert* in Expert Iteration. It grows in four steps, each gated by a matched-budget win over the previous one.

**S0 `legacy`** — today's pipeline GP: population 12, elites 4, ~7–8 generations at the default 64 evaluations (`search.rs:92-99`, `241-248`), uniform parent choice among survivors, alternating crossover/mutation by slot, one island by default (with `--islands > 1`, top-1 migration to every island every 4 generations). Kept forever as a control profile.

**S1 `modern_gp`** — the non-neural expert this spec makes the default (§8).

**S2 `gumbel_root`** — policy-guided root search with measured leaves:

```text
Input: incumbent p0, policy π_θ, value model v, budget n measured fits, m candidate edits
1. Sample m edits without replacement by Gumbel-top-k over logits(π_θ(·|s0)) + g_a
2. Phase j = 1..⌈log2 m⌉: give each surviving edit ⌊n / (⌈log2 m⌉ · |S_j|)⌋ fits,
   climbing the fidelity ladder F1 → F2 → F3 → F4 with phase j
   keep the top half by  g_a + logits_a + σ(q̂_a)  with  σ(q) = (c_visit + max_b N_b) · c_scale · q
3. Return the surviving edit; improved policy target
       π'(a) = softmax(logits_a + σ(completedQ_a)),  completedQ_a = q̂_a if measured else v_mix(s0)
```

Defaults from the Gumbel paper: `c_visit = 50`, `c_scale = 1.0`; `m = 16`, `n = 32` for the first experiments.

**S3 `sp_mcts`** — shallow single-player tree (depth ≤ 3 edits) with:

- **transpositions** keyed by canonical program SHA-256 (two edit orders that reach the same program share a node);
- **progressive widening** `|children(s)| ≤ ⌈k · N(s)^α⌉`, `k = 2`, `α = 0.5`;
- **expectimax backup**: chance nodes average over measured cuts, decision nodes take the max of child means — the right backup for a "best program found" objective, unlike two-player minimax;
- **leaf valuation** by the value model's *lower* quantile when deciding to stop and *upper* quantile when deciding to explore (optimism only where it is cheap).

`N(s)` counts planning visits. Reports always show **measured fits separately** from planning visits; a tree with 10,000 visits and 40 fits is a 40-fit search.

**Teacher-qualification gate (before any search output becomes a training label).** On a frozen set of training-role contexts not used for training: run the raw policy (sample K edits, measure, keep best) and the search at the **same measured-fit budget**. The search output is admissible as a distillation target only if the paired log-ratio improvement has a cluster-bootstrap 95% CI above zero (the `pools::bootstrap::cluster_bootstrap_ci` machinery already exists). Otherwise the round trains only the supervised heads.

### 6.7 G5 — League (deferred, with an entry criterion)

Snapshot every promoted policy. Start a league only when (a) ≥ 3 policies have been promoted under Gate F / SPQ-1, and (b) their paired payoff matrix on training contexts shows a non-transitive cycle or strong family specialisation. Until then, every evaluation compares a child against its parent, the strongest non-neural profile (S1 + retrieval), and a shuffled-context control — which is what SPQ-1 already encodes.

### 6.8 What each game trains

| Data source | Policy head (`π`) | Value / ranking heads | Cost per labelled example |
|---|---|---|---|
| G3 dreams | CE toward hidden program and behaviour-equivalent programs | value on known regret; calibration over difficulty | 1–2 fits |
| G1 + S1 GP runs on real train-role contexts | AWR on measured improvements; PKPO on seed sets | pairwise/ranking on measured pairs; cost/failure heads | 1 fit (shared across all heads) |
| G1 + S2/S3 search | CE toward qualified `π'` | value toward measured expectimax returns | search budget / m |
| G2 ranked reward | sign for RLOO/PKPO | `P(r=+1)` value target | free (a view) |
| Qualification (sealed/held-out) | **never** | **never** | — |

### 7.4 Effects, equality and leakage safety

A deliberately small effect lattice, enough to make leakage a compile error:

| Effect | Meaning | Ops (today) |
|---|---|---|
| `Pure` | function of `X` row only, no fitted state | `sigmoid`, `step`, `polar`, `norm_row`, `symlog`, `map`, `derive` |
| `FitX` | fitted on training `X` only | `standardize`, `robust_scale`, `rank`, `blom`, `quantile_bins`, `bin_onehot`, `winsor_clip`, `svd_project`, `nystrom`, `rff_*` (seeded), `impute` |
| `FitXY` | fitted on training `X` and `y` | `pair_prod`, `pair_ratio`, `target_bin_encode`, `select_corr`, all heads, `target` |
| `Ordered` | depends on row order | `lag`, `roll` (v3 ordered forms) |
| `Seeded` | uses an RNG stream | `rff_*`, `rand_proj`, `landmark_dist`, `nystrom` |

Rules enforced by the evaluator, not by convention:

1. A `FitXY` stage whose output feeds a **combinator that trains on predictions** (`stack`, `portfolio`, `boost`, `route` below) must be cross-fitted (K-fold out-of-fold) inside the combinator. The existing compact `portfolio` already does this; make it a typed rule.
2. `Ordered` stages are illegal unless the dataset declares `RowSemantics::Ordered` (the v3 rule at `advanced_v3/pipeline.rs:526-533`, applied everywhere).
3. `Seeded` stages carry their stream in the canonical text so replay is exact.

**Three kinds of sameness** (adopted from the rank-3 spec): `byte_identical` (same DSL bytes), `canonical_identical` (same canonical IR under the registry version), and `behaviour_similar` (fingerprints on the probe within the cut noise). Only the first two may collapse identities; the third only saves fits (§8.8).

And the benchmark uses a *different* engine (the feat-math lite race: population 32, tournament-3, crossover 0.25, three-rung successive halving; `evolve.rs:5307`). Product claims and benchmark claims therefore describe two algorithms.

### 8.2 `modern_gp`: the algorithm

A steady-state, **aging (regularized) evolution** core with lexicase parent selection, typed operators under a bandit, constant refinement, a quality-diversity archive and a fidelity ladder. Steady-state matters here: workers finish fits at different times, and aging evolution needs no generation barrier.

```text
modern_gp(context c, budget B fits, seeds S, policy π optional):
  P  ← population size  = clamp(⌊B/8⌋, 16, 64)
  T  ← tournament size  = max(2, ⌊P/8⌋)
  A  ← QD archive (cells §8.4), empty
  Q  ← FIFO population, oldest-first eviction

  generation 0 (fill to P, record propensities):
      incumbent (if any), bootstrap (pipe X (ridge)),
      retrieval top-k (k = min(8, P/4)) from the S3 history index,
      policy samples (k = min(8, P/4), if π available),
      QD-archive elites from the nearest prior contexts (k = min(4, P/8)),
      grammar-random for the rest (registry draw weights)
      → every member goes F0 → F1 → F2 (§8.3)

  repeat until budget, deadline or stall(max(32, B/4) fits without improvement):
      parent ← with prob 0.5: down-sampled ε-lexicase over case blocks (§8.5)
               else:          tournament of T sampled from Q by mean F2 RMSE
      arm    ← operator bandit (§8.6) | policy edit with prob ρ (start 0.25, bandit-tuned)
      child  ← apply(arm, parent [, donor from Q or A])     # typed, registry-legal
      if F0 rejects child: record, continue (no fit charged)
      measure child at F1; if fingerprint hits the FEC table: reuse, record fec_hit, continue
      measure child at F2
      every R = max(4, P/4) admitted children: Refine top-2 of Q (continuous params, LM/grid)
      push child to Q; evict oldest; update A; update bandit with the outcome
  promotion ladder: top ⌈P/4⌉ by F2 → F3 (3 cuts) → top 4 → F4 (all 5 cuts)
  return argmin F4 mean RMSE over {promoted} ∪ {incumbent}       (never worse than incumbent)
```

Defaults are proposals for the first preregistered comparison (EXP-019) and are recorded in the profile digest.

**Layering.** `jope-pipeline` must stay independent of the learning store (`seeds.rs:5`: "it never depends on the learning store"), and `jope-learning` already depends on `jope-pipeline`. So `modern_gp` does not query S3 itself. It accepts a `SeedSet` input — the existing `jope.pipeline-seeds/v1` file extended (v2) with a `source` (`incumbent | retrieval | policy | qd_archive | random`) and a propensity per seed — and a `ProposalSource` trait for in-search `PolicyEdit` calls. `jope-learning` (retrieval, QD archive) and `jope-policy` (KernelNet) implement them; the research CLI wires them together. The ≤ 4 seed cap of `seeds::admit` stays for `legacy`; `modern_gp` admits up to its generation-0 shares.

### 8.3 The fidelity ladder in the pipeline

| Rung | Cost | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| F0 static | ~0 | type/effect legality (§7.4); `MAX_WIDTH`; a cost-class bound from the registry |
| F1 probe | fit on ≤ 512 rows of cut-0 training, predict on a fixed 256-row probe | behaviour fingerprint (FEC, novelty); screening: drop if probe RMSE is worse than the population's 90th percentile by more than the probe noise |
| F2 | one cut, full rows (today's unit of work) | population fitness |
| F3 | three cuts | promotion screen with paired comparisons on the same cuts |
| F4 | all five cuts | the only rung allowed to name the episode champion |

This replaces "select on cut 0" with successive halving whose final decision always uses all cuts — the same principle as Lion's *funnel selection* (promote only what survives progressively larger proxies).

### 8.4 Quality-diversity archive

Cells are interpretable and few (the rank-1 and rank-5 specs' "2–4 axes" advice is right):

| Axis | Bins |
|---|---|
| head family | linear (`ridge`, `ridge_gcv`, `elasticnet`), tree (`histgbm`, `histgbm2`), kernel (`krr`, `gpr`, `ksmooth`), ensemble (`stack`, `ensemble*`, `portfolio`, `hurdle2`), combinator (`boost`, `route`), FM-based |
| feature width after transforms | log₂ buckets: ≤ d, ≤ 2d, ≤ 8d, ≤ 64d, > 64d (d = input columns) |
| target transform | none, `zscore`, `log1p`, `rank_gauss`, `winsor` |

6 × 5 × 5 = 150 cells per context. A cell keeps its best F2-or-better program; a newcomer replaces it if it is better by more than the cut noise. Across contexts, the archive is stored per context in S3 (§13) and retrieved by context similarity, so a cell's elite on a similar dataset becomes a generation-0 seed.

### 8.5 Lexicase over case blocks

Cases are residual blocks: the held-out rows of the measured cut(s) are hashed (seeded by dataset hash) into 16 blocks; a program's case error is its RMSE on each block. Down-sampled ε-lexicase (Hernandez et al., GECCO 2019): each selection event uses a random half of the blocks; ε per block is the median absolute deviation of block errors across the population (the existing `kernel/lexicase.rs` implements MAD-ε and down-sampling). This preserves specialists that win on part of the data — the raw material for `boost` and `route` combinators.

### 8.6 Operator bandit

Arms (all typed, all registry-legal). Build them on the existing `CanonicalMutationArm` set (`ParameterJitter`, `Insert`, `Delete`, `AdjacentSwap`, `HeadReplacement`, `Simplify`) and `jitter_params_v2`; what is new is the bandit, the homologous crossover and the combinator/motif/policy arms:

| Arm | Initial share | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| `SetParam` (log/logit-scale jitter for continuous; ±1, ±25% for integers, whichever is larger) | 0.25 | replaces ±2 integer steps |
| `Insert` (op drawn by registry weights, site uniform) | 0.15 | |
| `Delete` | 0.10 | |
| `Replace` (same `OpKind`, compatible width) | 0.10 | |
| `SetHead` | 0.10 | |
| homologous `Cross` (align stages by op kind and position; swap one aligned segment) | 0.15 | replaces one-point A[..i] ++ B[j..] |
| `Wrap-boost` (wrap parent as stage 1 of `boost`, stage 2 = small random pipeline) | 0.05 | new combinator (§7.5) |
| `Motif` (insert a validated motif) | 0.05 | only when the Library game has admitted motifs |
| `PolicyEdit` (KernelNet proposes an edit from residual tokens) | ρ | M2 onward |

Thompson sampling with Beta(1,1) priors over "child improves parent at F2 by more than the cut noise", contextualised by (head family, program length bucket); a 3% floor per arm keeps every arm identifiable; the chosen arm and its probability are logged for off-policy analysis.

### 8.7 Migration that keeps islands different

With `--islands I > 1`: ring topology; every `max(4, P/4)` admitted children, each island sends one individual sampled from its top quartile **weighted by behaviour distance from the receiving island's best**, which joins the receiver's population as the youngest member (subject to aging). Migrants are never re-fitted; the event is recorded as today.

### 8.8 Throughput: three changes that cost almost nothing

1. **Stage cache.** Call `feat_math::fit_with_stage_cache` (`model.rs:127`) from `jope-pipeline/src/search.rs::evaluate` with the cache key including cut, fold assignment and row digest. The cache already exists (`stage_cache.rs`: SHA-256 chain keys over exact stage encodings, 6 GiB cap, 64 shards).
2. **Functional-equivalence check (FEC).** AutoML-Zero's trick: hash the standardised F1 probe predictions (rounded to 6 significant digits) together with the head family; on a hit, reuse the recorded F2 result instead of fitting. Audit 2% of hits with a real F2 fit and report the false-hit rate; disable FEC for a context if it exceeds 1%.
3. **CPU-seconds, not only wall-seconds.** Record `getrusage` CPU time per fit in the pipeline receipt (today it records active wall seconds only), so budgets can be matched on CPU.

Preemption: fits whose F0 cost class is `Heavy` (`gpr` with large `m`, `fm*`, `histgbm` with many rounds) run in a child process under `jope-pipeline/src/execution.rs` with an `RLIMIT_CPU`, so a deadline is real and an overrun becomes a censored `timeout` outcome rather than an uninterruptible stall (`docs/pipeline.md:22-26` documents today's limitation).

### 8.9 One kernel, many profiles

`jope-pipeline` gains a `SearchProfile` value (`legacy`, `lite_race`, `kernel2-baseline`, `kernel2-experimental`, `modern_gp`, `gumbel_root`), each with a profile SHA-256 written into the receipt. `jope-bench --search-profile` calls the same code, so a benchmark row and a product run name the same algorithm. `legacy` and `lite_race` remain bit-reproducible controls.

## 9. Deep-learning design: KernelNet v2

### 9.1 What the current networks can and cannot see

Two empirical facts set the bar. The v1 q-head ranks fresh programs at chance (0.506 [0.441, 0.568], C76); and an earlier deep outcome ensemble trailed LightGBM on raw features (R² 0.690 vs 0.785, C78/C178). **No neural value model ships until it beats `ContextualSurrogateQHat` and a GBDT on the same leave-one-parent-cluster-out (LOPO) pools.**

### 9.2 Design principles

1. **Columns are tokens.** The decision "apply `symlog` to column 7" (or `(map expr="log1p(col(7))")`) needs column 7 to exist inside attention.
2. **Programs are trees with order.** Encode stage position, depth and parent links.
3. **Execution beats syntax.** A cheap probe fit gives a residual; the residual tells the policy what is still unexplained. This is execution-guided synthesis (Chen et al., ICLR 2019; Ellis et al., arXiv:1906.04604) and the neural analogue of semantic backpropagation.
4. **Memory beats parameters.** The ridge surrogate wins because it looks up neighbours. Give the network the same: attend over retrieved measured `(context, program, outcome)` triples.
5. **The value model is a PFN over experiment history.** Predicting a program's outcome from a few measured neighbours is in-context regression — the problem TabPFN solves for tables. Train it the way TabPFN was trained: on synthetic tasks from a prior. The Dream game (§6.4) *is* that prior.
6. **Size is not the bottleneck.** Start at 10–20 M parameters; scale only when held-out search AUC improves with size.

### 9.3 Architecture

```text
                    ┌───────────── Column tokens (≤ 256) ─────────────┐
 train-role X, y ──▶│ per column j: robust quantiles, missing rate,     │
 (cut-0 train rows) │ cardinality, skew, kurtosis, Spearman(x_j, y),     │──┐
                    │ spline-ridge R²(x_j → y), Hyperion per-row codes   │  │  ISAB set transformer
                    │ pooled per column; residual stats when available   │  │  (16 inducing points,
                    └────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘  │   4 layers, d = 256)
 existing 2,048 ctx vector ──▶ 3 global tokens (Hyperion / FM / target)  ───┤
 budget vector, n_rows, n_cols ──▶ 1 global token                         ───┤
                                                                             ▼
                    ┌───────────── Program tokens (≤ 128) ────────────┐   CONTEXT MEMORY  C
 current program ──▶│ canonical S-expression tokens + tree position     │──▶ program encoder
                    │ (stage index, depth, parent, sibling) + numeric   │    (4 layers, d = 256,
                    │ parameter embeddings (sign·log1p|v|, Fourier)     │     order-aware)       ─▶ P
                    └────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                    ┌───────────── Execution tokens (optional) ───────┐
 F1 probe result ──▶│ residual r = y − ŷ on ≤ 256 inner rows:           │──▶ appended to column
                    │ corr(r, x_j) per column, |r| vs ŷ slope, r        │    tokens + 1 summary
                    │ quantiles; behaviour fingerprint (64 probe preds) │    token               ─▶ E
                    └────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                    ┌───────────── Retrieved memory (k = 16) ─────────┐
 S3 history index ─▶│ nearest measured (context, program, g, fidelity)  │──▶ cross-attended as
                    │ by context-embedding cosine, train-role only      │    key/value tokens    ─▶ M
                    └────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                                                             ▼
                    Edit decoder: causal transformer (6 layers, d = 256), cross-attends [C; P; E; M]
                    Factorised action: family → site pointer (over P) → operator (registry mask)
                                       → parameters (binned + residual) → fidelity → stop
                    Heads on a pooled [C; P; E; M] summary:
                      value quantiles of g (τ = .1 … .9), P(ranked win), feasibility (ok / fit-fail /
                      score-fail / timeout), log-runtime quantiles (right-censored), QD-cell logits
```

Notes:

- **Full-program generation is the special case `p = ∅`.** The existing grammar automaton (`crates/jope-policy/src/grammar.rs`) remains the legality mask for whole-program decoding, so cold start is unchanged.
- **Column cap.** For datasets above 256 columns, keep the top 224 by spline-ridge R² with `y` and summarise the rest into 32 cluster tokens (k-means on column statistics). Record the selection in the receipt.
- **Leakage boundary.** Column statistics involving `y` are computed on cut-0 *training* rows only, the same rows the program will be fitted on. They never touch held-out rows of any cut, sealed test rows or `meta_test` families.
- **Two-tower screener** (for pools of thousands of candidates): a context tower and a program tower producing 256-d embeddings, scored by dot product, trained with in-batch softmax on measured winners. It ranks archives cheaply; the full model re-ranks the top 64. Embeddings can be stored as S3 Vectors (GA December 2025) for nearest-neighbour retrieval without a separate service (§13.9).

### 9.4 The residual-conditioned edit policy — boosting in program space

The single most important architectural change. After any `F1` probe measurement of the current program `p`:

```text
r      = y_inner − ŷ_p(X_inner)                         (inner rows of cut-0 training data)
ρ_j    = Spearman(r, x_j)            for every column j  (what is left to explain, and where)
η      = slope of |r| on ŷ_p                            (heteroscedasticity → target transform)
κ      = excess kurtosis of r                            (heavy tails → robust head / clipping)
```

These are appended to the column tokens. The policy then learns maps such as "large ρ on a column that `p` does not transform → insert a transform on it", "high η → `SetTarget(log1p)`", "residual has periodic structure in a time column → lag/rolling stage". It is what a human data scientist does after looking at a residual plot, and it is what makes the policy an *editor* rather than a *cataloguer*. The F1 probe costs a few percent of a full fit, and it is needed for behaviour fingerprints anyway (§8.8).

### 9.5 The value model as a PFN over the experiment history

The value heads answer: *what will `g` be if we measure program `q` on context `c` at fidelity `f`?* Make that an in-context-learning problem:

- **Support set**: the k = 16 retrieved neighbours `M` (measured triples on similar contexts) plus every measurement already made in this episode on `c` itself (`M_episode`).
- **Query**: `(c, q, f)`.
- **Prior for pre-training**: the Dream game. Each dream task gives dozens of measured programs on one context with a known noise floor; sampling support/query splits from them is exactly TabPFN-style prior-fitting.
- **Fine-tuning**: real train-role measurements from S3 (`GP_RESULTS_INDEX.json`, `gp_distilled/v1`), with LOPO splits so a parent cluster never appears in both support and query.

As the history grows, the model gets better *without retraining*, because more neighbours are retrieved — the property that makes the ridge surrogate improve across its five rounds (9,088 → 45,064 distilled rows).

### 9.6 Comparison ladder that every model change must climb

| Rung | Model | Must beat on LOPO shadow pools (pairwise acc, B=1/4/8 top-1 gain, ECE of quantiles) |
|---|---|---|
| R0 | `program_prior`, `ridge_program` | — |
| R1 | `ContextualSurrogateQHat` (C190) | R0 (already does) |

| R4 | KernelNet v2 with memory + execution tokens | R3, **and** in-search AUC at equal fits |

R2 is cheap and likely to land first; it becomes the default allocator if it wins.

### 10.2 The objective

For a batch drawn from the mixture in §10.6:

```text
L = 1.00 · L_π                                   policy (edit / program) loss, by data source
  + 0.50 · L_V                                   quantile value loss on g (9 quantiles)
  + 0.25 · L_pair                                noise-aware Bradley–Terry on measured pairs
  + 0.10 · L_feas                                4-class feasibility cross-entropy
  + 0.10 · L_cost                                right-censored log-runtime likelihood
  + 0.05 · L_cell                                QD-cell prediction (auxiliary, for diversity control)
  + β_t  · KL(π_θ ‖ π_anchor)                    only on real-data batches; β_t is a dual variable
```

The coefficients are starting values chosen to put each term's gradient norm within 0.3–3× of `L_π` at initialisation; log per-term gradient norms every 100 steps and re-balance once, by measurement, before the first preregistered run. They are frozen into the objective SHA-256 (the pattern `objective_sha256` in `dltrain/objective.py` already follows).

**`L_π` by source.**

| Source | Target | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Dreams (§6.4) | the hidden program's edit sequence, **or any program in its behaviour-equivalence set `E`** (fingerprint distance below the cut noise) | set-valued CE: `−log Σ_{t ∈ E} π_θ(t | s)` — do not punish the network for finding an equivalent program |
| Real measured edits | measured parent→child advantage `A = g(child) − g(parent) − b(s)`, with `b(s)` the value-head mean | AWR: `−clip(exp(A/τ), 0, 20) · log π_θ(a | s)`, `τ` = median absolute `A` of the batch |
| Qualified search output (§6.6) | improved policy `π'` (completed-Q) | `CE(π', π_θ)` |
| Generation-0 seed sets | K seeds per context; reward = best program GP reaches from the set | **PKPO** (Walder & Karkhanis, arXiv:2505.15201) with `k = K`: unbiased pass@k-style reward transform over the K samples, then RLOO baseline; plus a diversity term `−λ_D · log det(L_S + εI)` on the K seeds' program embeddings (DPP) |

The last row is the key change for generation 0. Seeds are consumed as a **set** by GP, so the objective must reward the best of the set and its diversity, not the average likelihood of each member. `rloo_weights` and `pkpo_weights` already exist as a pure library in `deploy/dl-flywheel/pipelines/rlfw/onpolicy/objective.py`; port them to `crates/jope-engine/src/training/objective_v2.rs` (PR 21) with differential tests against the Python on the committed fixtures (ADR-0006 P3 pattern).

**`L_V` — quantile value.** Target `G` = best `g` reached within the remaining budget along the measured trajectory (Monte-Carlo return), or the measured `g` of a dream oracle. Quantile Huber loss over `τ ∈ {0.1, …, 0.9}`, `κ = 1`. Use the 0.25 quantile for stop/promote decisions and the 0.75 quantile for exploration.

**`L_pair` — only learn differences larger than noise.** For measured pairs `(i, k)` on the same context: weight `w_ik = min(1, |Δg_ik| / (2·SE_ik))`, where `SE_ik` is the paired standard error across cuts (or the fidelity's noise prior when only one cut exists). Pairs inside the noise get weight → 0. The existing BT term weights by `|Δvalue|` but has no noise floor (`neural.rs:723-831`).

**`L_cost` — censored runtime.** `log t ~ Normal(μ, s)` from the head; uncensored rows contribute `log φ`, timeouts contribute `log(1 − Φ((log t_cap − μ)/s))`. Today runtime is `smooth_l1(log1p(runtime))` (`objective.rs:466-472`), which treats a timeout as an exact value.

### 10.3 Holding a KL budget: adaptive dual instead of a fixed λ

EI-1 and EI-3 show a fixed `λ_KL = 3.0` was not enough once `λ_ar` rose to 0.10 (KL stops at epoch 3; C186, C188). EI-2 kept `λ_KL = 3.0` and lowered `λ_ar` to 0.03 (C187); EI-4 raised the anchor to `λ_KL = 5.0` with `λ_ar = 0.025` (C189). Both completed four epochs without KL alarms, and neither reduced L6 against its matched control. Replace the fixed coefficient by dual ascent on a target:

```text
target KL budget κ per round (e.g. κ = 0.02 nats on the probe contexts, below the 0.05 stop)
after each logging window w:  β ← clip(β · exp(η_β · (KL̂_w − κ)/κ), β_min, β_max)
                              η_β = 0.5, β ∈ [1e-3, 100]
```

This is the PPO adaptive-KL controller applied to supervised continuation. The sticky ALARM/HOLD guard in `continuous_v6/kl_guard.py` stays as the hard stop.

### 10.4 Optimizer and schedule

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Optimizer | AdamW, β = (0.9, 0.95), ε = 1e-8 | β₂ = 0.95 is more stable for transformer fine-tuning on shifting data |
| Weight decay | 0.05 on 2-D weight matrices only; **0** on biases, LayerNorm, embeddings | fixes the "decay on every parameter" behaviour of the accepted trainer |
| Schedule | **Warmup–Stable–Decay**: 2% linear warmup → constant peak → decay branch | anytime checkpoints; children are short decayed branches off a stable trunk |
| Peak LR | 3e-4 for 12–18 M params (sweep {1e-4, 3e-4, 6e-4} once) | |
| Decay branch | last 10% of a child's steps, linear to 0.1 × peak | a child is evaluated from its decayed branch; the trunk continues at peak |
| Gradient clipping | global norm 1.0 | as today |
| Precision | BF16 autocast, FP32 master | as today |
| Optimizer state across rounds | carried with the trunk; if a fresh state is unavoidable, re-run warmup | avoids the fresh-AdamW shock in `warm_start.rs` |
| EMA | decay 0.999, served | |
| Ablation arms (one preregistered study) | Schedule-Free AdamW; Muon on hidden matrices + AdamW elsewhere | evidence that they help here, not elsewhere |

### 10.5 Continual learning without losing plasticity

Every promoted child is a warm start of its parent; after enough rounds the network stops learning (Dohare et al., *Nature* 2024). Three mechanisms, all cheap:

1. **Shrink-and-perturb at the start of each round** (Ash & Adams, arXiv:1910.08475): `θ ← λ_s·θ + σ_p·ε`, `ε ~ N(0, σ_init²)`, with `λ_s = 0.8`, `σ_p = 0.01`. Sweep `λ_s ∈ {0.6, 0.8, 1.0}` once.
2. **Head re-initialisation** every 5 rounds for value/cost/feasibility heads (cheap to relearn; most exposed to target drift).
3. **Generational re-distillation** every 10 rounds: initialise a fresh student, train it on the retained replay corpus (§13.4) plus the current teacher's soft policy and value targets, and enter it into the same promotion gate. If the fresh student wins, it becomes the new trunk. This also lets architecture changes enter without "surgery" on old checkpoints.

Plasticity monitors, logged per round: fraction of dead GELU units, effective rank of the penultimate features, and the gradient-norm ratio of new data to replay data.

### 10.6 Batch composition (the data diet)

| Stream | Share | Sampler |
|---|---|---|
| Dream tasks | 40% | PLR priority = positive value loss; staleness bonus; 10% uniform |
| Real fresh measurements (this round) | 20% | family-uniform (existing `family_uniform_champion_plus_seven_quantiles_v2` idea) |
| Real replay (retained corpus) | 20% | Horvitz–Thompson weights from the retention sampler (§13.4) so thinned history stays unbiased |
| Anchors: previously promoted champions and their measured siblings | 15% | uniform over families |
| Failures and timeouts (feasibility / cost heads only) | 5% | stratified by failure class |

Shares are logged per step and frozen into the objective SHA-256. A child whose dream share produces no real-context gain is rejected by the gate in §11, which protects against sim-to-real drift.

### 10.7 Why this should break the EI-0..EI-4 plateau — and the experiment that would prove it wrong

Hypotheses, each with a falsifying experiment of ≤ 1 GPU-day on the existing 1,148-context L6 panel:

| Hypothesis | Experiment | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|
| H-T (target): catalogue projection of out-of-catalogue winners is the problem | train the same small model with edit-level, open-support targets vs catalogue CE, equal steps | edit-level arm does not improve real inner-CV search AUC |
| H-K (KL control): fixed λ is the problem | adaptive β (κ = 0.02) at `λ_ar = 0.10` (the dose that tripped EI-1/EI-3) vs the EI-2 and EI-4 settings | the adaptive arm neither completes without a stop nor reduces L6 at equal KL |
| H-P (plasticity): warm starts are the problem | shrink-and-perturb vs plain warm start vs fresh init + distil | no difference in learning speed on fresh data |
| H-C (context): one prefix token is the problem | column-token encoder vs prefix token, same data | no gain in fresh-program pairwise accuracy |

These four are ordered cheapest first. All four are training-side changes that do not touch sealed data.

## 11. The flywheel protocol

### 11.1 Words that may be used, and when

Adopted from the rank-1 spec, tied to artefacts in this repository:

| Term | Means | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `archive_ratchet` | winners become next seeds; no network changes | `clover.selfplay-ratchet/v1` (exists; `policy_optimizer_updates: 0`) |
| `expert_iteration` | search output becomes training targets | a `jope.training-bundle/v1` whose targets come from a teacher that passed §6.6's gate |
| `learning_round` | a child checkpoint with `optimizer_updates > 0` trained from fresh, sealed round data | checkpoint manifest whose ancestry names the round's bundle; parameter SHA-256 differs from the parent |
| `self_play` | ≥ 2 consecutive learning rounds in which the child plays the next round, under ranked reward | round receipts chained by checkpoint ancestry |
| `qualified_self_improvement` | the child beats its parent **and** the strongest non-neural profile at equal resources on held-out evidence, preregistered | Gate F `promote-eligible` (F10 `accepted` for the step-60000 lineage) + SPQ-1 report with `margin_cleared`, both on pinned preregistrations |

### 11.2 Closure criteria for the first "self-play" claim

1. Checkpoint `P_t` proposes generation-0 seeds and edits inside `modern_gp`.
2. Episodes on train-role contexts and dream tasks produce sealed evidence (Parquet shard + manifest, §13.2).
3. `jope-engine` trains `P_{t+1}` from that evidence with `optimizer_updates > 0`; the checkpoint records the bundle SHA-256.
4. `P_{t+1}` changes behaviour on a fixed probe set (policy KL on 64 probe contexts > 0, within budget).
5. The Gate F arena judges `P_{t+1}` vs `P_t` on independent families (F10 on `meta_validation` cuts for the step-60000 lineage); SPQ-1 compares the *search* driven by each at equal fits and CPU-seconds.
6. The pointer moves only on acceptance, by conditional write; rejected children are retained as evidence.
7. Round `t+2` uses whichever checkpoint the pointer names.

### 11.3 ProgramDelta is not PolicyDelta

From the rank-3 spec, and the most important distinction in this section:

- **ProgramDelta** — one program beats another on a context. Search produces these every run. It is evidence about a *program*.
- **PolicyDelta** — a policy (with its search) produces better programs than another policy at equal resources across *new* contexts. It is evidence about the *learner*.

Every claim in a card (§14) names which one it is. A run of `modern_gp` that finds a better program is a ProgramDelta even if a neural network seeded it.

### 11.6 Fixes the existing self-play verifiers need before Round 1

1. **KL guard that can trip.** Under the preregistered tilt the guard is vacuous (§2.4 K4). 2. **`seed_cap_b`.** The preregistration's `B = 8` cannot run against `ELITES = 4`. Either preregister `B = 4`, or make `seed_cap_b` a `modern_gp` parameter (where generation 0 is larger, §8.2) and re-pin.
3. **Equal resources.** `equal_resource_across_rounds` is true only for round 0 today (`expansion.rs`); record CPU-seconds per arm and make equality a checked property, not a flag.

### 12.2 Measured versus predicted, in the type system

```rust
/// Produced only by an evaluator receipt verifier. No public constructor.
pub struct Measured<T> { value: T, receipt: ReceiptId }
/// Produced by any model. Never converts to Measured.
pub struct Predicted<T> { value: T, model: ModelId, quantile: Option<f32> }

pub trait EvidenceSink {          // ledgers, pointers, claims, training targets for value heads
    fn admit(&mut self, x: Measured<Outcome>) -> Result<()>;
}
pub trait PlanningOracle {        // search-time leaf valuation only
    fn value(&self, s: &State) -> Predicted<f64>;
}
```

`Measured<T>` has no `From<Predicted<T>>`, no `Default`, and is constructed only inside the receipt verifier module, so a surrogate value cannot reach a ledger by accident. Training targets for the value heads accept `Measured<Outcome>` (real runs) and `DreamOutcome` (synthetic, carrying its own provenance type) — never `Predicted`.

### 12.3 Identity rules

- Domain-separated SHA-256 over canonical bytes: `sha256("jope.<kind>/v<n>\0" ‖ canonical_bytes)`, using the existing canonical JSON (`jope-learning/src/canon.rs`: sorted keys, compact, shortest round-trip floats).
- A program's identity binds `registry_sha256`. Changing the registry does not rewrite old identities; it creates a new canonical version with a recorded transition (the ADR-0004 known-identity table pattern).
- Episode and bundle identities bind the profile digest, the policy checkpoint identity and the evaluator identity.

### 13.1 What is in S3 today, and what actually costs money

From `catalogs/s3.json` (inventory 2026-09-23, current versions only):

| Bucket | Objects | Size | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `veox-jopedime-datasets-use2` | 15,442 | 3.23 GB | `blobs/`, `catalog/`, `collections/`, `datasets/` |
| `veox-jopedime-learning-use2` | 645,281 | 430.65 GB | **all under `staging/`**: lineage-A encodings (419,644 objects, 149.84 GB), genebank (1.81 GB), preserved archives (141.78 GB), lineage-B `accepted60000-restored` (225,207 objects, 110.92 GB), mirror weights (26.30 GB) |
| `veox-jopedime-models-use2` | 967 | 3.93 GB | replicated to `models-usw2` |

| `veox-jopedime-archive-use2` | 180,547 | 33.08 GB | git bundles, node high-value staging |
| custody `…-dev` / `…-prod` / `…-sealed` | 186,305 / 186,304 / 8,679 | 118.1 / 117.8 / 246.1 GB | no object lock |

Total ≈ 953 GB in ≈ 1.22 M objects. All six `veox-` buckets have **versioning and object lock enabled, with no default retention configured**; the three custody buckets are versioned without object lock.

Three consequences that the reviewed specs missed:

1. **Storage dollars are not the constraint.** At the S3 Standard list price the repository's own retention tooling uses ($0.023/GB-month, `deploy/dl-flywheel/pipelines/rlfw_s3gp/s3_lifecycle.py`), the whole estate is ≈ $22/month even if nothing were in a colder class. The real costs are **signal density** for learning, **object count** (lifecycle transitions are billed per object, and S3 does not transition objects under 128 KiB by default since September 2024), **scan time** (the GP results tree stores one JSON object per result: building `GP_RESULTS_INDEX.json` over 18,380 runs is 18,380 GETs), and **evidence closure**.
2. **Deleting from a versioned bucket frees nothing by itself.** A `DELETE` writes a delete marker; the bytes stay as a non-current version until a `NoncurrentVersionExpiration` rule removes them, and object-lock retention can forbid that. Retention must be designed with lifecycle rules, not just delete calls.
3. **Nothing canonical had been written as of the inventory.** In the 2026-09-23 current-version inventory (which, as it states, does not establish historical absence) the learning mirror's `blobs/`, `hwm/`, `scores/` prefixes are absent and the replica is empty. There is no migration debt in the canonical namespace — only a design choice to make once.

### 13.2 Target layout (additive; `staging/` untouched)

```text
s3://veox-jopedime-learning-use2/

  research/v2/
    blobs/sha256/<aa>/<digest>                    # immutable content-addressed (existing mirror layout)

    bundles/<bundle_sha>/manifest.json                                       # jope.training-bundle/v1
    checkpoints/trunk/<ckpt_id>/…  checkpoints/children/<ckpt_id>/…

    capsules/<exp_id>.json                                                   # experiment-record/v2
    retention/plans/<plan_sha>.jsonl  retention/tombstones/<round>/…parquet
    indexes/<index_epoch>/…                                                  # derived, rebuildable
    pointers/{accepted-policy,trunk,serving}.json                            # conditional writes only
```

Rules:

- **Nothing below 128 KiB is written as a standalone object**, except pointers, manifests and ledger batches. Everything else is packed into Parquet (zstd) shards of 64–256 MiB — the `COMPACT` tier `jope_retention` already defines (≥ 64 MiB packs). No Parquet crate is in `Cargo.lock`, and `Cargo.lock` is itself an evaluator-identity input (`evaluation_v2.rs` hashes it), so shards are written by the in-repo minimal writer/reader (`jope-learning/src/retention/parquet.rs`: flat tables, PLAIN encoding, zstd pages), extended from one row group to many. Adopting the `parquet` crate later is an identity-transition PR (§15.2).
- Writes use the existing mirror semantics: `PutMode::Create` (`If-None-Match: *`) for immutable objects with readback; `PutMode::Update{e_tag, version}` for pointers (`jope-datasets/src/store.rs:252-372`); the round manifest is written last and is the commit point, exactly as `HEAD.json` is for ledgers (`jope-learning/src/sync.rs:109-129`).
- Rows carry `split_role`; the dream worker and the actors cannot read any key whose role is not `train` (IAM, §13.6).

### 13.3 Why Parquet rows, not JSON objects

A measured observation (program text, context id, cut, fidelity, RMSE per cut, CPU-seconds, failure class, fingerprint, policy id, propensity) compresses to roughly 0.1–0.3 KB in a zstd Parquet row. A round of §11.8 (≈ 350 K fits) is then ≈ 35–100 MB — one to two shards. A thousand rounds fit in under 100 GB. **Store every observation; never store bulk predictions or fitted states for non-champions** (they are recomputable, §13.5).

### 13.4 Value-based retention: thinning history as the system gets smarter

The user requirement is to clear out low-value history *as the system learns*. The design goal is stronger: **prune without biasing anything that learns from or reports on the history.** The tool is survey sampling.

**Units.** Retention decisions are made per *episode* (all observations of one search on one context), so within-context pairwise and ranking losses stay valid; frontier rows inside a dropped episode are kept separately.

**Always kept (inclusion probability π = 1).**

| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Authority (T0) | ledgers, pointers, preregistrations, judge reports, promotion decisions, claim-bound receipts, sealed-look registry, schemas, retention plans and tombstones |
| Frontier | the best program per `(context, cut)` (the HWM), Pareto-optimal `(RMSE, CPU-seconds)` programs per context, QD cell elites |
| Learning lineage | every row in a training bundle of the accepted trunk or the serving checkpoint |
| Failure exemplars | the first 8 rows per `(failure class, context family)` per round |

**Everything else: value score → inclusion probability.**

```text
For an episode e at retention round t:
  surprise(e)   = mean over its rows of |g_measured − ĝ_t| / σ̂_t      # value model at round t; high = still teaches
  rarity(e)     = 1 / density of e's (head family, op-set) signature in the retained corpus
  recency(e)    = exp(−(t − t_e)/H),  H = 10 rounds
  V_t(e)        = w_s·surprise + w_r·rarity + w_c·recency               # weights in the plan, e.g. (0.6, 0.3, 0.1)
  π_t(e)        = min(π_{t−1}(e), max(π_min, κ_t · V_t(e)))              # monotone: never re-include
  keep e        iff  u(e) < π_t(e),   u(e) = hash(e.id, plan_seed) / 2^64  # deterministic, auditable
Store π_t(e) in the episode's rows (column retention_pi) and in the tombstone for dropped episodes.
```

- `π_min = 0.02` keeps a uniform floor of every kind of history.
- `κ_t` is solved each round to meet a row budget (e.g. "retain at most 5 M episodes in the hot corpus").
- Because `π` only decreases, the probability that an episode survives to round `t` is exactly `π_t(e)`; the **Horvitz–Thompson weight `1/π_t(e)`** makes any mean over the retained corpus an unbiased estimate of the mean over the full history (Horvitz & Thompson, 1952). The training sampler (§10.6, "real replay" stream) and every offline statistic use those weights.
- "As the system gets smarter": episodes the current value model already predicts well (low surprise), in crowded signatures, and old, lose inclusion probability round after round. Surprising, rare and recent episodes stay. That is the data-pruning result of Sorscher et al. (arXiv:2206.14486) — keep the hard, informative examples — made unbiased.

**Mechanics on a versioned, object-locked bucket.**

1. `jope_retention plan` (extends the existing read-only `classify`) computes `π_t`, writes `retention/plans/<plan_sha>.jsonl` (`jope.retention-sample/v1` rows) and a summary; it changes nothing.
2. `jope_retention apply --plan <sha> --signed-by <key>` (new) rewrites each affected shard with only the kept rows, reads it back and verifies the row SHA-256s, commits a tombstone shard listing dropped episode IDs with their `π`, then deletes the superseded shard (a delete marker).
3. A lifecycle rule on `research/v2/{episodes,dreams,decisions}/` expires **non-current** versions after 30 days, which is what actually frees the bytes. Do **not** set a bucket-level default retention: it would apply to every prefix, including the shards that thinning must be able to expire. Instead the writer sets per-object retention (`x-amz-object-lock-mode: GOVERNANCE` plus a retain-until date) on T0 objects only (`ledgers/`, `pointers/`, `rounds/`, `bundles/` of accepted lineages, `capsules/`, `retention/`). New pointer versions remain writable under retention; only deletion of retained versions is blocked.
4. The `EXPIRE` action that `jope_retention` never emits today gets one new rule, `E6: superseded-by-verified-thinned-rewrite`, which requires all three of its existing conditions (no training value under the plan, a proven rule, an agreeing independent check) plus the signed plan.

### 13.5 Recompute, don't store

The evaluator is deterministic under a pinned identity (ADR-0004). So for non-champion programs keep only a **reconstruction seed** — `(program canonical text, dataset hash, cut, fold seed, evaluator identity, registry SHA-256, toolchain digest)`, ≈ 0.5 KB — and drop fitted states, predictions and per-candidate emitted code after 14 days. A weekly restore drill recomputes 20 random seeds from the retained rows and compares RMSE and fingerprint bit-for-bit (or to the declared tolerance); a failure halts retention `apply`.

### 14.4 Living meta-analysis: beliefs that accumulate

Each hypothesis keeps a running random-effects estimate across every wave that measured its endpoint:

```text
for hypothesis H with per-wave effects (y_i, v_i) on a common endpoint (log-ratio scale):
  fixed weights      w_i = 1 / v_i
  heterogeneity      Q = Σ w_i (y_i − ȳ_w)²,   τ² = max(0, (Q − (k−1)) / (Σw_i − Σw_i²/Σw_i))   (DerSimonian–Laird)
  pooled             μ̂ = Σ w*_i y_i / Σ w*_i,  w*_i = 1/(v_i + τ²),  SE = (Σ w*_i)^(-1/2)
  status machine     PROPOSED → SUPPORTED (CI excludes 0, ≥2 waves, one preregistered)
                              → QUALIFIED (a sealed/held-out preregistered wave agrees)
                              → CONTRADICTED / RETIRED
Waves without a CI contribute only as "direction votes" in a separate sign test and never move μ̂.
```

A belief row reads like this, and is the only thing an agent needs to know about seeding:

```text
H-SEED-RETRIEVAL  retrieval-seeded GP beats ordinary GP  |  μ̂ = −0.035 (no CI: 1 wave, unequal attempts)
                  status PROPOSED (SUPPORTED needs a CI, ≥ 2 waves, one preregistered)  |  waves: EXP-008  |  next: EXP-021
```

Lane discipline: only `development_confirmatory` and `sealed_qualification` waves may move a hypothesis to QUALIFIED; `research_adaptive` waves can move it at most to SUPPORTED.

### 14.5 `STATE.md` — the 2,000-token entry point

Generated sections, in this order, each with a byte budget:

1. **Pins** (300 B): HEAD, accepted policy identity, evaluator identity, S3 canonical prefix.
2. **What is established** (1,600 B): QUALIFIED/SUPPORTED beliefs, one line each.
3. **What is open** (1,600 B): PROPOSED beliefs ranked by expected information gain (pooled SE × decision relevance).
4. **Do not repeat** (1,200 B): every `do_not_repeat_unless` across cards, deduplicated.
5. **Last five waves** (1,500 B): M0 lines.
6. **Live blockers** (1,000 B): open P0 items (#48, #62, the unpinned SPQ-1 prereg fields).

`AGENTS.md` then says: read `docs/STATE.md` first; read `MASTER_PLAN.md` only when changing direction. That reduces the start-up reading from ~145 K tokens to ~3 K for most tasks.

### 14.7 Making it useful to AI agents, not just smaller

- **Retrieval by question.** `jope-records experiments ask --hypothesis H-SEED-RETRIEVAL` prints the belief row, its contributing cards and the next experiment. `--operator log1p` prints every card whose effects involve an operator.
- **Embeddings.** Card text embeddings can be indexed in S3 Vectors under `research/v2/vectors/cards/` for semantic search across thousands of waves. The embedding is a finding aid; the card is the answer.
- **Measured usefulness.** One preregistered study (§17, EXP-032): agents given `STATE.md` + cards vs. agents given the raw documents answer 30 fixed questions about past results. Score correctness, citation validity and tokens used.

## 18. Risks, kill criteria and anti-patterns

### 18.1 Risk register

| Risk | Early signal | Mitigation | Kill / rollback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dreams do not transfer** (sim-to-real gap) | dream-trained child improves dream regret but not real inner-CV search | 30% misspecified hidden programs; real train-role `X`; every child judged on real families | if EXP-024 shows no real lift, cap the dream stream at 10% and redirect it to value-model pretraining only |
| **Leakage through context tokens** | column statistics with `y` computed on wrong rows | cut-0 training rows only; permuted-label canary must yield chance-level policy preferences | any canary failure quarantines the round |
| **Winner's curse** | F2 winners regress at F4 | F4 required to name a champion; paired cuts | none needed — structural |
| **FEC false hits** | audited hit with different F2 result | 2% audit; per-context disable above 1% | disable FEC globally if > 1% overall |
| **Ranked-reward non-stationarity** | win-rate oscillates | buffer snapshots hashed; α schedule with hysteresis | freeze α |
| **Policy monoculture** | generation-0 diversity collapses | floors: retrieval ≥ 25%, grammar-random ≥ 25% of slots; DPP term; QD archive seeds | raise floors; drop `PolicyEdit` share to the bandit minimum |
| **KL or entropy collapse in training** | KL above budget; entropy below floor | adaptive β (§10.3); sticky HOLD | reject child |
| **Throughput too low for dreams + episodes** | round exceeds its wall budget | stage cache, FEC, small `X` for dreams, heavy-fit child processes | shrink dream count; never shrink the judge |
| **Evaluator-identity churn** | many small identity transitions | one transition per release (§15.2) | revert the release's refactor PR |
| **Retention deletes something needed** | restore drill fails; a claim source missing | T0 classes, signed plans, 30-day non-current expiry, tombstones with π | stop `apply`; restore from non-current versions within 30 days |
| **Foundation-model licence or cost** | licence review negative; CPU class too heavy | `fm_feat` uses in-repo Starlight first; external FMs stay `Research` | keep FM primitives out of `Default` admission |
| **Over-claiming** | prose ahead of receipts | claim ladder (§11.1); compiled cards; Gate F refuses development rosters | the card generator refuses unresolvable numbers |
| **Compute theatre** | tree visits reported as effort | measured fits reported separately everywhere | — |
| **Goodhart on L6** | L6 improves, search does not | L6 is a safety panel, never the promotion endpoint | — |

### 18.2 Anti-patterns this spec forbids

1. Calling the archive ratchet "self-play".
2. Distilling a search that has not beaten the raw policy at equal measured cost.
3. Using a surrogate score where a measured value is required (enforced by types, §12.2).
4. Choosing a champion on one cut.
5. Training on any record whose role is not `train`.
6. Deleting from a versioned bucket and calling it freed storage.
7. Writing one JSON object per observation to S3.
8. Hand-editing a summary that a compiler owns.
9. Adding a new search loop instead of a profile.
10. Adding Python to the product or the trainer.


## 19. The demonstration: showing algorithmic self-play credibly

A live page (an artefact built from receipts, never from hand-entered numbers) with four panels, each replayable from S3 by round ID:

1. **Dream recovery.** A hidden program is sampled; the agent sees only `(X, y)`. The KL bound in K4: with within-block logits `η·a_i`, `a_i ∈ [−A_max, A_max]`, the logit span is at most `Δ = 2ηA_max = 0.8`. For two-level logits (a fraction `f` of leaves at the top of the span, the rest at the bottom) and `Z = f·e^Δ + 1 − f`, `KL(f) = f·(e^Δ/Z)·ln(e^Δ/Z) + (1−f)·(1/Z)·ln(1/Z)`, maximised at `f ≈ 0.369` with `KL ≈ 0.0793` nats. ## Appendix B. Golden45 gap analysis used in §2.3

Every row of `scores.csv.zst` carries `benchmark_valid=True`; the README's benchmark-valid view additionally drops the 45 test-selected `jope-dime` rows named in `assets/regression/protocol-corrections-tabular-regression-v2.json`. The script applies the same correction. (A reader-side check; PR-01 moves this into `jope-records baseline_facts` in Rust.)

```python
import csv, io, json, math, statistics as st, collections, zstandard
f = "assets/regression/sotarena-tabular-regression-v2/results/tabular-regression-v2/scores.csv.zst"
corr = json.load(open("assets/regression/protocol-corrections-tabular-regression-v2.json"))["corrections"]
def corrected(r):
    return any(r["method_id"] == c["method_id"] and
               (r["dataset_id"] in c.get("dataset_ids", []) or r["source_revision"] == c["source_revision"])
               for c in corr if not c["benchmark_valid"])
rows = csv.DictReader(io.StringIO(zstandard.ZstdDecompressor().stream_reader(open(f, "rb")).read().decode()))
by, dropped = collections.defaultdict(dict), 0
for r in rows:
    if r["status"] != "ok": continue
    if corrected(r): dropped += 1; continue
    by[r["dataset_id"]][r["method_id"]] = float(r["value"])
me, fm = "jope-dime-regressor", "tabfm_ensemble_regressor"
gap, rank, vs_fm = [], [], []
for m in by.values():
    if me not in m: continue
    best = min(v for v in m.values() if v > 0)
    gap.append(math.log(max(m[me], 1e-12) / best))
    rank.append(1 + sum(v < m[me] for v in m.values()))
    if fm in m and m[fm] > 0: vs_fm.append(math.log(m[me] / m[fm]))
print(dropped, len(gap), round(st.median(gap), 4), sum(g > .3 for g in gap), sum(r > 100 for r in rank),
      sum(r <= 10 for r in rank), round(st.median(vs_fm), 3), sum(x < 0 for x in vs_fm))
```
