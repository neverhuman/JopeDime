\section{\texorpdfstring{\EZ{}}{EurekaZero}: Learning Taste}\label{sec:method}

Referee-scored lists define reference targets for a program writer and
separate ranking map. Proposed game day freezes the writer for one
proposal. \Cref{sec:price} sets the cost boundary; the inspected operating loop in
\cref{sec:method-status} instead updates an adapter to rank rule-based GP proposals.

\subsection{Reading the board}\label{sub:method-board}

The reference regression encoder maps the board (\cref{sec:game}) to
\num{2048} numbers, $z_p=\encP(p)$, in three blocks: a row-order-independent
summary of pretrained row embeddings (512 values); a projected hidden
state from a pretrained third-party tabular regressor reading training
rows and labels (\num{1024}; third-party provenance pending; restricted; redistribution prohibited);
and a summary of two 32-element training-target windows scaled to $[0,1]$
(512 values; constant targets map to zero).

Preparation caps the combined tabular splits at \num{50000} rows. Encoder
inputs remain training-side only: the regressor block samples at most
768 context rows and 256 query rows from those training rows. Frozen
meta-training cohort moments standardize each component block before
concatenation and elementwise clipping to $[-32,32]$.

The reference encoder is fixed and hash-pinned during writer training.
Encoding has a 30\,s cap per problem; non-finite contexts are refused,
not imputed (\cref{app:training}). Final held-out labels and derived
statistics must stay outside its training-side input closure. Declared
non-use of final targets establishes neither closure nor numerical
qualification. Accepted-policy use of CPU contexts requires replay in the native (Rust) implementation
and bounded-execution \mbox{receipts}.

Whether similar best programs imply nearby problems needs a separate
representation study, reserved in \mbox{\cref{sub:results-ablation}}.

\subsection{Describing the move}\label{sub:method-move}

A move is one program in the typed language of \cref{sec:game}, with a
401-token training inventory listing 31 transforms and 22 prediction heads; the product runtime restricts executable heads.
The reference writer emits a flat operator-and-slot stream with fixed
start, engine and regression-task tokens: no reasoning or parenthesis
tokens or \texttt{pipe} wrapper. Its nine-phase grammar mask permits
up to six transform stages and three expression stages; each expression
tree is bounded at depth four and 15 nodes
\citep{kusner2017grammar,dai2018sdvae}. After the end token, detokenization
renders a parenthesized program; it must parse to enter the pool.
The mask does not check remaining token budget: a draw without an end token
at the 128-token horizon, including the fixed header, is discarded as
incomplete. Grammar legality establishes neither execution success nor
quality. The pool removes exact decoded-string duplicates and records
their SHA-256; the executor records canonical recipe hashes. Probe-based
behavior matching is audited separately from pool deduplication; without
probe evidence, behavioral novelty is unknown. Readable Python and Rust exports are a reference requirement.
Legacy recipe emitters reject the synthesized input stage; the separate
fitted-state path is allowlisted. Reported winners still require the parity
check in \cref{sub:method-referee}.

\subsection{One map for problems and programs}\label{sub:method-map}

The reference map ranks programs within a problem by decreasing logit.
It reads a program as $z_w=\encS(w)$, the layer-normalized mean of
non-padding token embeddings, including \texttt{BOS}, the fixed engine and
task header, and \texttt{EOS}. Pooling discards token order, limiting its
ability to distinguish \mbox{compositions} built from the same tokens.

The map's own two-layer context network projects the \num{2048}-value $z_p$
to $e_z\in\mathbb{R}^{512}$. Three 512-value blocks form the interaction:
$z_w$ as is, $z_w\odot\tanh(W_\gamma e_z+b_\gamma)$ and
$|z_w-(W_{\mathrm{proj}}e_z+b_{\mathrm{proj}})|$.
Four two-layer heads read this \num{1536}-value vector; their mean is the
ranking logit $\mapv(z_p,z_w)$. Reference ranking losses supervise the mean
jointly, without separate targets or bootstrap samples. Each ranking head
uses dropout of 0.05 during training. The runtime head reads $[e_z,z_w]$;
the program-only control reads $z_w$.

The logit is uncalibrated: cross-problem comparisons, error estimates and
verified-win probabilities need separate evidence. The referee establishes
validity and measures performance.

Map training uses scored lists $G_p$ for problem $p$, with percentile
$\rho_w\in[0,1]$: 1 denotes the best. Native training requires at least
eight scored programs per source list and samples eight per comparison:
the champion and seven quantile-stratified comparators. The lower-level
listwise loss accepts groups of at least three with finite percentile
targets (\cref{app:training}). The list-level form trains the map's softmax
toward a target concentrated on the best
\citep{cao2007listnet}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{map}}(p)=-\sum_{w\in G_p}
  \frac{e^{\rho_w/\tau}}{\sum_{u\in G_p}e^{\rho_u/\tau}}\,
  \log\frac{e^{\mapv(z_p,z_w)}}{\sum_{u\in G_p}e^{\mapv(z_p,z_u)}},
  \label{eq:mapLoss}
\end{equation}
with native target temperature $\tau=0.25$. Target percentiles are scaled;
mean ranking logits enter the prediction softmax unscaled. A unique best
program receives all target mass in the low-temperature limit. This
within-problem listwise ranking objective does not establish CLIP-style
alignment across problems and programs; contrastive learning
\citep{oord2018cpc,radford2021clip} is an analogy. A Bradley--Terry term
compares pairs on the same lists \citep{bradley1952rank}. Stillborn moves
have no score, so the objective covers only programs that run.

The program-only head controls for ranking without problem context
(\cref{sub:results-ablation}). Ranking valid scored programs alone does not
test the validity rate $q_{\mathrm{run}}$ in \cref{sub:price-bill}.

\begin{keyidea}
The reference game-day protocol omits the ranking map. Matched first
proposals therefore test whether training improved the program writer.
\end{keyidea}

The native reference writer and map use separate context networks and
token embeddings. Ranking neither drops drafts nor screens the writer.
One native GP route assigns map-derived parent weights to ranked
survivors by program identity; the bounded driver cycles among up to
four parents chosen by measured loss. Neither establishes trained-map
lift; \mbox{evaluation must} bind the exact search route.

Search-parent weights govern search; writer-loss weights use referee rank
in training. Whether map-guided targets improve later proposals needs
the matched ablation in \cref{sub:results-ablation}; historical diagnostics
in \cref{fig:fw-target-progress,fig:fw-target-history} cannot supply it.

\Cref{sub:method-selfplay} takes these referee-scored lists as proposed
offline \mbox{training} targets.

\subsection{The self-play loop}\label{sub:method-selfplay}
% Algorithm 1 is defined at the start of the subsection so that the full-width
% float can reach the top of the next page.
\begin{algorithm*}[t]
\caption{\textbf{The reference self-play loop (training).}}\label{alg:selfplay}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require training problems; serving writer $\writer$ and map $\mapv$; incumbents $\inc{p}$
\For{each round}
  \For{each sampled training problem $p$}
    \State $z_p \gets \encP(p)$; keep the first 64 distinct parseable draws from $\writer(\cdot\mid z_p)$ \Comment{$T=1$; bounded fallback if short}
    \LongState{verified search, seeded with the 64 drafts and 16 tail seeds (teachers/fallbacks and random exploration), \mbox{proposes more programs}}{search as teacher}
    \State $\referee$ fits and scores each distinct program on 5 inner folds \Comment{stillborn: no score}
    \State \textbf{if} a program strictly beats $\inc{p}$ \textbf{then} it becomes $\inc{p}$ \Comment{board ratchet}
    \State add every scored program to the list $G_p$ \Comment{winners and also-rans}
  \EndFor
  \State train a challenger $(\theta',\psi')$ on the lists
  \State \textbf{if} it beats $\writer$ on held-out cuts \textbf{then} $(\theta,\psi)\gets(\theta',\psi')$ \Comment{proposed player ratchet}
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm*}

The native proposal sampler seeks 64 distinct parseable programs at $T=1$
in at most eight batches of 128 neural draws (\num{1024} total). A shortfall
triggers up to \num{10000} seeded random canonical pipelines projected
through the grammar; an incomplete candidate pool is refused. These
training-search budgets are separate from the \mbox{single-draw game-day}
protocol in \cref{sub:method-gameday}.

Only the 64 neural or fallback seeds receive normalized rank weights;
the 16 tail seeds are unscored. Unscored survivors inherit the lowest
scored weight while any scored remain.
A \emph{cut} fixes a dataset split and evaluation case; \emph{teachers} are programs retained from earlier searches. Native assembly's 16-seed
tail takes up to ten same-cut teachers, then cross-cut teachers; random
fallbacks fill to ten, random exploration the rest. Standalone seeding uses
16 random programs. Teacher-first execution prioritizes retained cut teachers;
\cref{alg:selfplay} leaves the tail route-dependent.

The reference design uses expert iteration \citep{anthony2017expert},
combining a writer loss weighted by referee rank on $G_p$ with map and
auxiliary losses (\cref{app:training}):
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{writer}}(p) &= -\sum_{w\in G_p}\bigl(0.05+\rho_w^{4}\bigr)\log\writer(w\mid z_p),
  \label{eq:writerLoss}\\
  \mathcal{L} &= \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{writer}} + 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{map}}
  + 0.25\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BT}} \nonumber\\
  &\quad + 0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{runtime}} + 0.05\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{prog}}.
  \label{eq:totalLoss}
\end{align}
\Cref{eq:writerLoss} is schematic: it shows referee-rank weights. The native
loss also smooths grammar-legal token targets, averages token losses per
sampled program and normalizes by total \mbox{program weights}
(\cref{app:training}).

Here $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BT}}$ is a Bradley--Terry loss on the same lists,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{runtime}}$ a Huber loss between $\ln(1+s)$ and
$\ln(1+\operatorname{softplus}(h))$, where $s$ is nonnegative measured
seconds and $h$ is the raw runtime-head output, and
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{prog}}$ the Bradley--Terry loss of the program-only control head.
A median-ranked program carries about a tenth of the top-ranked program's
weight in the writer loss. Its referee score also contributes to the map's
ranking targets.

The reference has two ratchets (\cref{sub:game-referee}): a program must
strictly beat the board's incumbent; proposed writer admission uses 16
held-out cuts with three seeds each. A challenger must have a favorable
mean log error ratio, win at least as many cuts as it loses and fail no
more often to replace the serving writer.
%
The admission cases are fixed before testing. For each cut, take the natural
log of paired best-error ratios (challenger divided by serving), average
over matched seeds, then average across cuts. Negative values favor the
challenger. This admission test remains separate from the single-program
comparison in \cref{sub:results-learning}.

\paragraph{The historical reference.} Its decoder-only writer is separate
from the ranking and auxiliary heads. \Cref{app:training} inventories the
architecture, registered capacity, token allocation and training corpus.

Architecture specifications do not qualify a checkpoint or runtime
(\cref{sub:results-protocol}). The historical reference covers tabular
regression only; native training currently refuses numerical dispatch.
CPU float32 (Candle) and GPU BF16 (tch) inference are not yet shown to agree numerically; random streams and uniform-token fallback differ.
This record does not establish that \Cref{alg:selfplay}'s proposed player-admission ratchet has run.



\subsection{Keeping the referee honest}\label{sub:method-referee}

Reliable training targets require a referee that resists exploitation.
In February 2025 Sakana AI walked back
claims after its AI CUDA Engineer exploited the evaluation code \citep{sakana2025walkback}. In June
2025 METR reported frontier models patching their scorers \citep{metr2025rewardhacking}, and 16\% of
\num{1968} agent-benchmark tasks \mbox{can be passed} without being solved \citep{zhong2026hackerfixer}.

The protocol must withstand such attacks. Selection, promotion and final
scoring require separate data (inner folds, held-out cuts and a final fold
scored once), with sealed meta-test families outside learner inputs. The
proposed comparisons also require seccomp and Landlock sandboxing and
memory, swap and time limits. Both a planted label leak and a sealed canary
must be caught. Every reported winner must be re-run from emitted code,
with native and emitted predictions agreeing within
$10^{-9}\max(1,|\hat{y}|)$.

One earlier leaderboard entry for our search selected each dataset's best
test-scored outcome from a ledger of \num{63108} evaluations; we exclude it as test selection (\cref{app:incidents}).

\subsection{Game day: \texorpdfstring{\searchone}{search = 1}}\label{sub:method-gameday}

The proposed game-day protocol fixes the components and seed before one
regression draw, retaining failures and all incurred costs
(\cref{alg:gameday,box:searchone}). \Cref{sub:results-learning} reserves the
matched comparison with a frozen control writer.

\begin{algorithm}[!htb]
\caption{\textbf{Proposed game-day protocol (\searchone).}}\label{alg:gameday}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require new regression problem $p$ with training rows and metric; frozen $\encP$ and $\writer$
\State $z_p \gets \encP(p)$ \Comment{read the board}
\State $w \sim \writer(\cdot\mid z_p)$, one draw at $T=1$ \Comment{one move; no retry}
\State $f \gets \textsc{Fit}(w,\ \text{training rows})$ \Comment{as any algorithm is fit}
\State $\hat{y} \gets f(\text{new rows})$ \Comment{a stillborn $w$ scores as a loss}
\State \Return $w,\ \hat{y}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Report later search separately, as \citet{silver2017mastering} reported
AlphaGo Zero's raw policy network apart \mbox{from its lookahead} search (\cref{sub:results-search}).
