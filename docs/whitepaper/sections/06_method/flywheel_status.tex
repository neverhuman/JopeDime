The retained CSVs summarize a later regression admission record (\cref{fig:fw-validation}), separate
from the historical reference above. An adapter is the trainable
adjustment evaluated by this admission loop. Utility is the logged
scalar score, with larger values treated as better.
The admission CSV records rounds, candidate and best validation
utilities, acceptance flags and timestamps. The tables omit the
adapter architecture, score normalization and evaluation population,
so they do not support comparisons with other models.
The source inspection that follows describes the loop implementation.
It does not yet bind that source or the adapter's identity to the
recorded run. Logged utilities alone do not identify the snapshot's
relationship to the historical writer or map.

In the inspected loop implementation, heuristic program edits, retrieval
and grammar sampling supply candidate programs. Only an adapter that ranks
proposals by predicted cost is optimized; the encoder and other modules
stay frozen. This loop has no neural program writer. Its pool-utility
calculation uses the recorded scores and gate outcomes of
the first four candidates as revealed history, then selects the candidate
with the lowest predicted cost from the pool, including those entries.
This is a conditional ranking task, separate from a single new program drawn by the
historical writer.
Training puts the lowest recorded scores first in its slates; utility
evaluation uses stored pool order. The two calls can therefore reveal
different examples.

The inspected caller supplies modality tokens and a mask while zeroing
separate context and prior inputs. Its objective combines regression to
recorded costs and listwise matching, with a fixed target for
failed gates. The reporting code averages the negative selected scores,
assigning a fixed penalty to failed gates.

Candidate programs are evaluated on two training-side folds; the historical
reference procedure specifies five. Evaluations are logged separately from
training-pool admission: ordinary admission requires passing gates and a
recorded score below the training context's incumbent by more than $10^{-6}$.
Historical programs have a separate admission path.
The adapter's default gate can accept equal utility; rejection restores
model and optimizer states.

Lower predicted cost is preferred. Logged utility averages the selected
programs' recorded outcomes; it is not the adapter's cost prediction.
For new proposals, the caller uses predicted costs and an exploration
setting to select programs for exact evaluation. The referee still decides
validity and improvement. Logging an evaluation does not itself add the
program to the adapter's training pool.

The admission prefix covers \num{1889} consecutive rounds, 775--2663.
Four rows are marked accepted: two strict best increases and two ties.
The recorded best remains 1.1378. After the accepted tie at round 882,
all \num{1781} subsequent rounds are rejected (\cref{fig:fw-validation}; see \cref{app:acceptance-over-rounds} for the isolated recorded-best trajectory and cumulative acceptance rate).
Comparisons across harnesses need matched data, normalization and scoring
protocols.

The older detailed summaries cover only 25 of those rounds. In that subset,
training utility exceeds validation utility by 0.282--0.290
(\cref{fig:fw-trainval}). This is a difference between reported aggregates
on separate training and validation contexts; it does not isolate a cause
or establish behavior in the missing summaries. The late validation plateau
is evidence to explain, rather than proof of useful transfer. Encoder
integrity and parameter updates require their own receipts; the plotted
utility tables do not establish either.

Historical GP target scores provide a separate view in
\cref{fig:fw-target-progress}. The 159 repeated dataset/cut/arm groups cover
55 distinct dataset/cut pairs; the 64 groups with lower final scores cover
23 pairs. Shared incumbent and teacher seeds make the arms dependent.
These retained development outcomes do not establish that learning improved
the proposal policy.

\Cref{fig:fw-target-history} follows one historical dataset/cut, chosen
after inspection for the largest policy-seeded relative improvement in
the preceding comparison set. Every reported generation is shown, without
applying a cumulative minimum. All three arms finish at the same value;
the policy-seeded arm first records it one generation after the controls.
Shared seeds prevent treating their matching outcomes as independent discoveries.
