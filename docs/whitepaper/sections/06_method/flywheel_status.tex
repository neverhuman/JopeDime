\subsection{A separate adapter admission record}\label{sec:method-status}


The retained CSVs summarize a later regression admission record (\cref{fig:fw-latest-admissions}), separate
from the historical reference above. An adapter is the trainable
adjustment evaluated by this admission loop. Utility is the logged
scalar score, with larger values treated as better.
The admission CSV records rounds, candidate and best validation
utilities, acceptance flags and timestamps. The tables omit the
adapter architecture, score normalization and evaluation population,
so they do not support comparisons with other models.
The source inspection that follows describes the loop implementation.
It does not yet bind that source or the adapter's identity to the
recorded run. Logged utilities alone do not identify the snapshot's
relationship to the historical writer or map.

In the inspected loop implementation, heuristic program edits, retrieval
and grammar sampling supply candidate programs. Only an adapter that ranks
proposals by predicted cost is optimized; the encoder and other modules
stay frozen. This loop has no neural program writer. Its pool-utility
calculation uses the recorded scores and gate outcomes of
the first four candidates as revealed history, then selects the candidate
with the lowest predicted cost from the pool, including those entries.
This is a conditional ranking task, separate from a single new program drawn by the
historical writer.
Training puts the lowest recorded scores first in its slates; utility
evaluation uses stored pool order. The two calls can therefore reveal
different examples.

The inspected caller supplies modality tokens and a mask while zeroing
separate context and prior inputs. Its objective combines regression to
recorded costs and listwise matching, with a fixed target for
failed gates. The reporting code averages the negative selected scores,
assigning a fixed penalty to failed gates.

Candidate programs are evaluated on two training-side folds; the historical
reference procedure specifies five. Evaluations are logged separately from
training-pool admission: ordinary admission requires passing gates and a
recorded score below the training context's incumbent by more than $10^{-6}$.
Historical programs have a separate admission path.
The adapter's default gate can accept equal utility; rejection restores
model and optimizer states.

\paragraph{One retained admission ledger.}
\Cref{fig:fw-latest-admissions} covers all \num{2164} captured rounds,
775--2938: six admissions and \num{2158} rejections. Strict gains occur at
rounds 785, 859 and 2750; exact ties occur at 782, 882 and 2795.
The first \num{1889} rows end at round 2663. The final 275 add the gain at
2750 and tie at 2795. Round 2750 raises retained utility from 1.1378 to
1.1384 (rounded), an increase of about $+\num{5.73e-4}$. A change in recorded best-round identity alone
is not a utility gain. \Cref{fig:fw-admission-margins} separates those
outcomes using candidate minus prior retained-best utility;
\cref{app:latest-admissions} gives the margin distribution for this same
ledger. The 101 records added after round 2837 are all rejections;
the retained-best utility is unchanged through round 2938.

Program-incumbent events count improvements to training programs, whereas
adapter admissions concern the ranking adjustment.
\Cref{fig:fw-promotions-per-hour} puts the former on a time scale for
rounds 2644--2663; its paired utility panel records no adapter admission.
These outcomes need separate denominators and cannot stand in for one another.

\Cref{fig:fw-round-throughput} measures distinct training contexts divided
by full completed-round wall time in rounds 2696--2715. It includes work
and waits inside each round; it is neither deployment throughput nor a
count of distinct tasks across rounds. The operating windows differ from
the full admission prefix, so these panels do not form a matched learning curve.



The remaining windows, alternative summaries and historical GP comparisons
are collected in \cref{app:flywheel-telemetry,app:record-windows}.
A later operating window in \cref{app:latest-event-rates,fig:fw-latest-event-rates}
puts reported program-event counts and rates on their logged elapsed-time
axis. These measure search activity during training. \Cref{sec:results}
asks a different question: whether a frozen writer's first proposal
improves under a matched comparison. Admission and event rates do not
answer that test.
