\subsection{Evaluation contract}\label{sub:results-protocol}

Freeze the writer, permitted task inputs, output support, decoding rule and
budgets before opening final outcomes. Pin dataset versions and content
hashes, row splits and family assignments. Separate unseen problem
families from training and development, and report which task types were
actually tested.

Report known training and pretraining exposure for the writer, encoders
and learned comparators, with unresolved provenance marked explicitly.
Bound unseen-family claims to the exclusions actually established.

Each \EZ{} submission follows \cref{alg:gameday}, counting failed generations
and executions in the overall win rate. Also report validity and wins among
valid attempts; neither rate alone establishes learning. Pair outcomes
and uncertainty by family, with encoding, generation, fitting and prediction
costs. Disclose internal model or hyperparameter selection within the emitted procedure.
\TBD{Document the final problem inventory, dataset identities, row splits,
family assignments and exclusions, component provenance, evaluator,
checkpoint, seeds, task metrics and failure accounting.}

opening final outcomes. Hold the encoder, program grammar and output
support, permitted task inputs, decoding rules and fitting protocol,
referee, incumbent and resource budgets constant; vary the writer checkpoint. Changes to the
support, encoder or evaluator belong in separate conditions.

Pair the first draws under predeclared seeds and report all of them, with
no selection of the best seed or candidate. Report paired family-level
outcomes, failures and costs alongside source-bound evidence of optimizer

\subsection{Earlier development diagnostics}\label{sub:results-diagnostics}
Closed-out development studies evaluate a frozen reference adapter and its
modifications, rather than the writer's independent first proposals.
The first three studies use 1,219 development-training contexts from
205 families and three seeds with different five-way family partitions.
Prior fitting and gradient-clipping continuations exclude evaluation and
selection families. Test-time adaptation uses only the four revealed
candidates in each context and resets its weights after scoring.
Fits or settings were locked before outer-fold evaluation.
Contexts are averaged within families; families receive equal weight.
Utility-gain intervals use 10,000 paired-family bootstrap draws shared
across seeds and summarize the median seed gain. Reported intervals are
95\%; $p$ values are one-sided, with one primary contrast per study.
These studies reuse a development population; they are not independent
replications.

\href{https://github.com/neverhuman/JopeDime/blob/717f6e272ef258e9c38c88504eb76c7faf3f2530/research/flywheel_selfplay/h195_blend025_kernel_confirm/README.md}{\textbf{Calibrated blend.}}
A calibrated tree prior receives weight $0.25$ in a cost-space blend with
the reference adapter. Its primary endpoint is the maximum utility among
four revealed and four lowest-cost unrevealed candidates, rather than a
single native choice. The gain is $+0.0137$ (95\% interval
$[+0.0056,+0.0259]$; $p=0.0005$ against a $+0.001$ margin).
Elo is $1523.1$ $[1513.5,1532.4]$, relative to the reference at $1500$.
Family harm below $-0.01$ is $7.3\%$ ($15/205$).
The result is a \textbf{candidate}, with no adoption and zero adapter
optimizer updates; the tree prior was fitted.

\href{https://github.com/neverhuman/JopeDime/blob/717f6e272ef258e9c38c88504eb76c7faf3f2530/research/flywheel_selfplay/h197_pcgc/README.md}{\textbf{Per-context clipping.}}
Clipping each context's gradient before averaging, with 40 updates per
trajectory, gives utility gain $-0.02510$
($[-0.05128,+0.00009]$; $p=0.9743$; $0/3$ positive seeds).
Elo is $1485.63$ (lift $-14.37$, 95\% interval $[-24.34,-4.38]$).
Family harm below zero is $46.83\%$, and the treatment does not strictly
beat its shuffled-factor placebo: \textbf{NO\_ADVANCE}.

\href{https://github.com/neverhuman/JopeDime/blob/717f6e272ef258e9c38c88504eb76c7faf3f2530/research/flywheel_selfplay/h198_ttt/README.md}{\textbf{Revealed-only adaptation.}}
Selection on a separate panel chose one SGD step at learning rate
$10^{-5}$ in all $15/15$ cells. At that locked setting, adaptation
changes scores in $1{,}219/1{,}219$ contexts but changes no selected
program. The largest grid budgets flip only $1$--$4$ of $1{,}219$ choices.
Utility gain is $0.000000$, with interval $[0.000000,0.000000]$.
Elo is $1500.00$, with interval $[1500.00,1500.00]$.
The verdict is \textbf{NO\_ADVANCE (inert treatment)} within the tested
one-to-five-step, $10^{-5}$--$10^{-4}$ learning-rate grid; it does not
exclude effective adaptation at larger budgets.

\href{https://github.com/neverhuman/JopeDime/blob/717f6e272ef258e9c38c88504eb76c7faf3f2530/research/flywheel_selfplay/h201_strict_admission/README.md}{\textbf{Admission audit.}}
The sole primary attempt is \textbf{INCOMPLETE}, with formal verdict
\textbf{INCONCLUSIVE}: one of $91$ choices in $19$ development-validation
families differs from its archived choice, failing the native parity gate.
Post-failure descriptive replay of a stricter admission rule accepts
$0/2{,}418$ historical proposals. This replay neither qualifies the
checkpoint nor establishes future power or sequential error control.

The single-choice studies use the selected candidate's utility, with
invalid choices assigned $-2$. Their endpoint differs from the blend's
best-of-eight endpoint; the reported Elo values are specific to each
protocol. Checkpoints, cohort identities, seeds and locks are pinned by
the linked reports and their accompanying protocol and metrics records.

None of these closeouts adopts a checkpoint or establishes learning of
independent first proposals, deployment throughput or unseen-family
transfer. The final comparisons above and their interpretation in
\cref{sec:meaning} remain pending.
