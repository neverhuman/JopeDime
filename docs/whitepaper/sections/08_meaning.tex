\section{Discussion}\label{sec:meaning}

The comparisons in \cref{sec:results} test whether training improves
first proposals on independent new families. Their outcomes will bound the
claims below. Development records alone cannot establish
transfer, useful new composition or autonomous scientific
discovery.

\subsection{Learning to select and learning to invent}

A better task-dependent choice from a fixed program archive demonstrates
selection. A useful composition absent from the training archive is a stronger
result, and requires an output space that permits it. Token-by-token
decoding can produce either.

The inspected loop has no neural program writer
(\cref{sec:method-status}). Its training-program admissions therefore
cannot establish learning by a writer, even when the admitted program
differs from its predecessors.

Report the admissible output space and training archive. Compare the evaluated checkpoint's outputs with its training archive to
distinguish the two claims. Absence from an archive establishes novelty
relative to that archive, rather than to the scientific literature.
Different spellings can express the same computation. Claims of invention
therefore need comparisons of program behavior and known methods,
alongside predictive quality.

\subsection{Moving judgment across problems}

If learning improved first proposals on new families, it would support the
hypothesis that training evaluations can be retained as a transferable
proposal capability. It would not show that one proposal wins on every
problem. Test-time search may improve the program returned for a task.
Weigh its value against the cost of training a better writer.
The search curves and full cost account in
\cref{sub:results-search} bound this tradeoff.

\subsection{The referee defines the boundary}

The framework optimizes what its referee measures. Repeated development
comparisons can overfit that referee. A restricted language can hide
useful algorithms from the learner. Admission on a higher development
score records a selection decision. It does not quantify uncertainty or
guarantee improvement in the target population.

and cost evidence. Analogy to a game supplies neither. Report observed
failures, distribution shifts and remaining referee tests with that
evaluation (\cref{sub:results-failures}).

\subsection{A bounded claim about self-improvement}

Improving an external algorithm, the proposer and the rate at which it
learns are distinct claims. Evidence for the first does not establish the
others. An optimizer trace with verified parameter changes can establish
that training occurred. A lower training objective measures the fitting
criterion. Neither alone shows that first proposals improved on unseen
families.

People still choose the task, metric, representation and resource limits.
Any claim must stay within the level supported by
\cref{sec:results}; stronger claims require further evidence.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sub:meaning-limits}

Four design limits remain beyond the pending evaluation.
The reference boards are tabular prediction tasks. Other task types need
their own qualified encoding, language and protocol
(\cref{sub:game-boards}).

The program language bounds proposals,
and its primitives encode human prior knowledge (\cref{sub:game-board}).

The referee is noisy and can be gamed. Strict improvement on inner folds
does not quantify confidence (\cref{sub:game-played,sub:method-referee}).

The economics are modeled rather than measured. Pilot costs and the point
at which training repays itself remain unreported
(\cref{sec:price,sub:results-search}).

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

% Trace: AlphaGo Zero, cited in §1 and §3.3 (silver2017mastering).
Within a game, a learner with a referee went beyond the best human play.
We make the analogous question testable: can the same recipe improve the
first algorithm a system proposes for a new problem? Invention becomes a
game with a board, a move, a referee and an incumbent opponent.
Its price is the cost of a verified win. \EZ{} is designed to train a
grammar-constrained writer and a separate problem--program map on targets
from verified search, and to deploy one program per problem. We specify
the design, cost model and evaluation protocol. Retained development
records show admission and search behavior, not transfer.
% Trace: §6.8 (figures/data/admissions.csv, rounds.csv) and §7.7
% (sections/06_method/sources/01M674E8XA0S4ZS5BT6FVH5QKX.md).

The decisive experiment is a single comparison between the frozen reference
writer and the same writer after a predeclared training intervention.
Each would submit one proposal on problem families held out from both
(\cref{sub:results-learning}). Engineered intelligence, in our sense, is a
capability whose aim, judge and memory are designed to be audited.
\Cref{sec:results} must show whether experience improves the first guess.
