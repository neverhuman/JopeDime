\section{Discussion}\label{sec:meaning}

What would better first proposals on independent new families establish,
and where would that claim stop? This section states, for each outcome of
\cref{sec:results}, which claim it would support. The recorded training
trajectory alone cannot establish transfer, useful new composition or
autonomous scientific discovery.

\subsection{Learning to select and learning to invent}

A better task-dependent choice from a fixed program archive demonstrates
selection. A useful composition absent from the training archive is a stronger
result, and requires an output space that permits it. Token-by-token
decoding can produce either, so report the admissible output space, the
training archive and the relation of each proposed program to that archive. Absence from an archive establishes
novelty relative to that archive, rather than to the scientific literature.
Different program spellings can also express the same computation. Claims
of invention therefore need comparisons of program behavior and known
methods, alongside predictive quality. The evaluated checkpoint's outputs,
compared with its training archive, decide which of the two claims applies.

\subsection{Moving judgment across problems}

If learning improved first proposals on new families, that would support the
hypothesis that training evaluations can be retained as a transferable
proposal capability. It would not show that one proposal wins on every
problem. Test-time search may still improve a learned writer; its value
should be weighed against the training cost of improving the writer itself.
The measured search curves and full cost account of
\cref{sub:results-search} bound how far this claim extends.

\subsection{The referee defines the boundary}

The framework optimizes what its referee measures. Repeated development
comparisons can overfit that referee, while a restricted language can hide
useful algorithms from the learner. An admission based on a higher
development score records a selection decision. It does not quantify
uncertainty or guarantee improvement in the target population.

\subsection{A bounded claim about self-improvement}

Improving an external algorithm, improving the proposer and improving the
rate at which the proposer learns are distinct claims. Evidence for the
first does not establish the others. An optimizer trace with verified
parameter changes can establish that training occurred; a lower training
objective measures the fitting criterion. Neither alone shows that first
proposals improved on unseen families.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sub:meaning-limits}

Beyond the pending evaluation, four limits belong to the design itself.
The reference boards are tabular prediction tasks; other task types need
their own qualified encoding, language and protocol
(\cref{sub:game-boards}). The program language bounds what can be proposed,
and its primitives encode human prior knowledge (\cref{sub:game-board}).
The referee is noisy and can be gamed, and strict improvement on inner
folds does not quantify confidence (\cref{sub:game-played,sub:method-referee}).
The economics are modeled rather than measured: pilot costs, and the point
at which training repays itself, are still to be reported
(\cref{sec:price,sub:results-search}).

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

% Trace: AlphaGo Zero, cited in §1 and §3.3 (silver2017mastering).
Within a game, a learner with a referee went beyond the best human play.
We asked whether the same recipe can improve the first algorithm a system
proposes for a new problem, and made the question testable. Invention
becomes a game with a board, a move, a referee and an incumbent opponent.
Its price is the cost of a verified win. \EZ{} is designed to train a
grammar-constrained writer and a separate problem--program map on targets
from verified search, and to deploy one program per problem. The design,
the cost model and the evaluation protocol are specified here. The
retained development records show admission and search behavior, not
transfer.

The decisive experiment is a single comparison: the frozen reference
writer against the same writer after a predeclared training intervention,
one proposal each, on problem families held out from both
(\cref{sub:results-learning}). Engineered intelligence, in our sense, is a
capability whose aim, judge and memory are designed to be audited. Whether
experience can also make its first guess better is what \cref{sec:results}
must show.
