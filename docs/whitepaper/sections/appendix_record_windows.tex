The main text selects four views of admission decisions and operating
activity. The consolidated figures here retain the earlier windows and
historical search summaries without changing any underlying observations.
\Cref{fig:fw-validation} covers the 775--2663 admission prefix, while
\cref{fig:fw-telemetry} (with train--validation aggregates in
\cref{fig:fw-trainval}) resolves the 25 retained detailed summaries.
Gaps between windows are not filled or treated as observations.

Lower predicted cost is preferred. Logged utility averages the selected
programs' recorded outcomes; it is not the adapter's cost prediction.
For new proposals, the caller uses predicted costs and an exploration
setting to select programs for exact evaluation. The referee still decides
validity and improvement. Logging an evaluation does not itself add the
program to the adapter's training pool.

\Cref{fig:fw-validation} shows the earlier 775--2663 prefix of the unified
ledger in \cref{sec:method-status}; \cref{app:acceptance-over-rounds}
provides its acceptance-rate view. Comparisons across harnesses require
matched data, normalization and scoring protocols.

The older detailed summaries cover only 25 of those rounds. In that subset,
training utility exceeds validation utility by 0.282--0.290
(\cref{fig:fw-trainval}). This is a difference between reported aggregates
on separate training and validation contexts; it does not isolate a cause
or establish behavior in the missing summaries. The late validation plateau
is evidence to explain, rather than proof of useful transfer. Encoder
integrity and parameter updates require their own receipts; the plotted
utility tables do not establish either.
\Cref{fig:fw-telemetry} shows the loop's operating measurements from the earlier
retained window.

\paragraph{Retained operating windows.}
Across the five 20-round program-event slices, rounds 2005--2024 contain 15
events (\cref{fig:fw-recent-development}), 2082--2101 contain 10
(\cref{fig:fw-followup-development}), 2343--2362 contain 22
(\cref{fig:fw-later-development}), 2508--2527 contain 15
(\cref{fig:fw-subsequent-window}), and 2644--2663 contain 21
(\cref{fig:fw-program-promotions}). Each records zero adapter admissions.
The later 20-round throughput slice, 2696--2715, also records none; it
reports contexts and wall time rather than a program-event total.
Completed-time, event-distribution and rate summaries appear in
\cref{app:development-time,app:event-counts,app:subsequent-rates,app:promotions-per-hour,app:round-throughput}.
The window separation and scope follow \cref{sec:method-status}; logged
round time includes work and qualification waits.

For the adapter record in \cref{fig:fw-validation}, the right panel of
\cref{fig:fw-admission-shortfall} summarizes candidate gaps to each round's
recorded best. Each retained round contributes once; the curve does not
count independent tasks or distinct programs.

Historical GP target scores provide a separate view in the first panel of
\cref{fig:fw-target-progress}. The 159 repeated dataset/cut/arm groups cover
55 distinct dataset/cut pairs; the 64 groups with lower final scores cover
23 pairs. Shared incumbent and teacher seeds make the arms dependent.
Scope: \cref{sec:method-status}.

The remaining three panels of \cref{fig:fw-target-history} follow one
historical dataset/cut, chosen after inspection for the largest
policy-seeded relative improvement in the preceding comparison set. Every
reported generation is shown, without applying a cumulative minimum. All
three arms finish at the same value; the policy-seeded arm first records it
one generation after the controls. Shared seeds prevent treating their
matching outcomes as independent discoveries.
