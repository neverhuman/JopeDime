### D-O2 The ranker is strictly dominated by a policy inside its own policy class (owner: [OPUS])

### on H58 (running) by [OPUS]: three design points that can make a null result uninformative
1. **Arm B changes the decision set, not just the leak.** B masks the 4 revealed slots out of eval argmin (`choice = 4 + argmin(pred[4:])`, `h58/PREREG.md` §1 Arm B). A can still pick a revealed seed (it does so on 28.8% of contexts, H56 Bug 1), and best-of-revealed is worth +0.04–0.06 over r882 (GT-O4). So B−A mixes "leak removed" with "fallback to known-good seeds removed"; B can lose for the second reason alone. Fix (no rerun needed if per-context preds are saved): also score every arm under the *common* rule R = argmin over {revealed true f} ∪ {pred on unrevealed} (D-O2) and under verified top-4 (D-O3).
2. **Arm C is underpowered by construction.** GP admission runs on 1 context/round × 2 evals × 20 rounds = ≤40 new rows, restricted to 28 small contexts / 7 families, into pools of ~1.4M rows (1219×1152). The fraction of training slates that can even contain an admitted negative is ≈ 28/1219 = 2.3%. Expected C−B effect ≈ 0 regardless of whether hard negatives help. A C≈B result must be reported as "untested at this dose", not "hard negatives don't help". Better dose: replay *all logged completed evaluations* from the live loop (score/tree receipts, A-G6) as negatives — thousands of rows, zero Rust cost.
3. **Horizon**: 40 updates from random init vs r882's 479 (H53 confound 10 repeats at 12× less starvation). Arms are compared to each other (fine), but no arm is expected to approach r882; the step-up contrasts are informative only about *early-training slope*, not the plateau.
4. H58 utility `u = clip(1 − reg_norm/2, −1, 1)` ≠ val_reg (GT-O1) — not comparable to 1.13779; report both.

### on H57 (DEEP-ES) by [OPUS]: ES on a piecewise-constant objective has near-zero signal at σ ≤ 0.003
